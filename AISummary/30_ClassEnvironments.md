## 1. The Five Primary Gameplay Environments

Campaign balance and class design are tuned across five core operational environments:
*(Note: Atmospheric **Level 1: Pure Aither** and **Level 6: The Deep / The Mist** are extreme environmental hazards and narrative setpieces; they are **NOT tracked or tuned for class balance**).*

1. **Civilized Society (The Spire Interior / Civil Wards):**
   - **Rarity & Multi-Group Escalation (Never 1-on-1):** Open combat within the Spire is exceptionally rare. Any violent conflict (PC vs. Anything Else) **will almost never stay one group on one group**: noise and visual chaos instantly alert the ward. Within moments, whistle-blowing citizens, **Ward Constables**, and potentially even **The King's Guard** arrive on scene in overwhelming force, transforming any fight into a multi-faction crisis where escape and de-escalation are the only viable victories.
   - High-society districts, commercial wards, residential tenements, and administrative courts *within* the Spire structure (Wards 1–15).
   - **Strict Civil Disarmament & Direct-Exit Surveillance Laws:**
     - **Everyday Armor (Padded & Leather Only — Must Resemble Normal Wear):** Civilian armor is capped at padded cloth and leather, and **must look as close to normal civilian clothing as possible**. Visibly combat-styled clothing and **most things with clockwork attachments** are **not allowed**—though **over-garments can hide material changes** (allowing concealed *Niracrest/Mitheral Weave* or *Ebon/Gildrod Leather* linings under normal clothes).
     - **Daily-Carry Utility Dagger (Mundane Tool Only — The "Carrying with Intent" Reality):** Citizens may legally carry a common utility knife/dagger (`1d4`, 2 sp mundane steel only), but it **cannot** be made of rare combat materials or have clockwork modifications. In practice, **most common citizens will never draw or use the dagger in a fight out of sheer terror of being prosecuted for "Carrying with Intent."** Self-defense claims by commoners are aggressively dismissed by constables; winning a brawl is seldom worth being sent to penal sump-mines, so street fights are almost universally fought with fists, boots, or clubs.
      - **The 'Rotten Apple' Underworld Reality (Why Gangs Enforce Disarmament):** Concealed weapons (sword canes, hidden blades) are suicidal liabilities in the urban underworld. A street thug caught with a sword cane risks bringing down the King's Guard on crime bosses three tiers above them—and when the authorities move against illegal arms, *'the entire barrel is thrown away for one rotten apple.'* The fastest way to disappear or be expelled from any syndicate is risking group status. If a crime boss discovers an underling packing a sword cane, that underling will wake up framed and serving **15 to 20 years of forced labor in the penal sump-mines** for crimes they had nothing to do with, sacrificed instantly to preserve the syndicate's survival.
     - **Dueling Swords & Noble Impunity (The "Fair Steel" Mandate):** Only **Nobles** and **Wall Merchants** (and commissioned officers / King's Guard) may openly carry and use dueling swords/rapiers. While commoners face arrest for using any weapon, the law inherently assumes that when an aristocrat draws a rapier, it is lawful defense of rank. However, to qualify, a dueling blade **must be standard, unenhanced mundane steel/iron**. If it incorporates **exotic performance materials (*Niracrest*, *Mitheral*) or clockwork modifications**, **it is by definition NOT a dueling blade**, but an illegal mechanized weapon of war, stripping the owner of immunity and bringing peer disgrace. Civil Spire swords are capped at mundane `1d6` output, keeping parlor fights balanced against **Pugilists** (`1d6` fists) and **Survivors** (`1d4` daggers).
     > [!IMPORTANT]
          > [!IMPORTANT]
     > **SPIRE WEAPON LAWS — ABSOLUTE CANON MANDATE:**
     > Inside the Civil Spire wards, weapons are strictly governed by two universal rules:
     > 1. **Universal Civilian Rule:** Only **Fists (unarmed strikes)** and **1d4 mundane utility knives/daggers** are permitted. (Even then, commoners avoid drawing knives out of fear of "Carrying with Intent" prosecution).
     > 2. **The Single Exception:** **Wall Merchants, Nobles, Great Nobles, and their personal guards/retainers** are legally permitted to carry a **1d6 dueling blade** (rapier, sabre, or shortsword).
     >    - **Strict Material & Modification Limit:** **No enhancements of any kind**—no exotic materials (*Niracrest*, *Mitheral*), no magic runes, and **no clockwork abilities/modifications**. Any modification automatically classifies the weapon as an illegal weapon of war, stripping the owner of their legal exemption and triggering immediate King's Guard intervention.
     >    - **NO OTHER EXCEPTIONS:** Class feats, traits, concealed holsters, disguised canes, or alternative weapons (clubs, truncheons, wrenches, cutlasses, sledges) are strictly prohibited in Civil Spire wards.

2. **High Radiant Environments (Grids, Warrens & Defended Encampments):**
   - Spire defense battlements, the **Warrens**, and **defended encampments (civilian and military)** anchored by dedicated dynamos/field generators, or areas within ~50 feet of an active primary dynamo.
   - **Extreme In-World Rarity:** Saturated High-Radiant combat is **exceptionally rare in actual play**. High-Power Converter modules are legally locked in armories, strictly regulated under *King's Rite*, and heavily monitored. Players only engage in Tier 1 combat during desperate Spire-siege defenses or assaults directly inside sovereign dynamo cores.
   - **Zero Disarmament Laws in Warrens & Encampments:** Unlike interior civil wards, the Warrens and exterior defended encampments enforce **no weapon, armor, or clockwork restrictions**. Full plate, longarms, powered exoskeletons, and unboxed ordnance operate openly alongside saturated wireless induction.
   - **Saturated Wireless Induction & True Siege Calibers:** Calibrated high-radiant weapons draw unlimited energy from the ambient air (**`1d12` to `2d12` damage, reaching `3d10` for Great Spire Cannons**, cycling 1 round between shots), high-draw force barriers function continuously, and heavy technical rigs achieve maximum output. Combat classes lose the damage and durability race to technical classes here, but such encounters represent a fraction of the campaign.

3. **Low Radiant Environments (Perimeters, Walled Grounds, SkyShips & 4-Mile Buffer):**
   - Areas extending **2 to 3+ times the radius of High Radiant cores**, the **walled grounds/yards outside the Spire proper (within the Outer Wall)**, **SkyShips cruising in the Zone**, and **within ~4 miles of New Avalon's outer walls**.
   - **Relaxed / Captain-Governed Weapon Rules:** Outside the Spire proper (even within the Outer Wall), weapon/armor bans are usually not enforced (workers carry dual-purpose axes; those with money carry pistols). **Aboard Airships, the only rules that exist are those the Captain chooses to and can enforce** (allowing Riflemen, Mad Bombers, Engineers, and Soldiers to carry full combat gear when the Captain permits).
   - **Wireless Induction Output (`1d10` to `2d10`):** Operates at standard stepped-down capacity (–1 damage die size/count compared to High-Radiance, –10 to –20 ft range reduction); cartridge weapons, martial arms, and aerial boarding maneuvers share the spotlight.
   - **Combat Class Competitiveness:** Dedicated combat classes (**Soldiers, Scouts, Pugilists**) remain fully competitive here: their `+2` to `+3` attack bonus advantage, heavy armor AC (+3 to +5 AC lead over technical classes), shield blocks, and 35–60% higher HP wound buffers match or beat the stepped-down energy damage of technical specialists who must expend their entire AP pool to keep pace.

4. **The Neither: No Radiant Power (Wilderness, Outlands & Subterranean Depths):**
   - Surface lowlands, sunken trenches, wild valleys, and subterranean shafts beyond 5× generator distance.
   - **Hard Technological Limit & Grind Attrition:** Wireless radiant coupling is completely dead (0 inductive uptake). Portable **Cartridge Weapons** operate at full range (`30–150 ft`) and high burst damage (`1d8` to `2d10`), but are strictly limited by **finite cartridge scarcity across the 70% attrition grind** and `Reload 1 / 2 / 3` action costs.
   - **Head-to-Head Martial Dominance:** Dedicated Martials (**Soldiers, Scouts, Pugilists**) dominate extended outland expeditions thanks to 35–60% higher HP (larger 3-Step Wound buffers), higher AC and Shields (shutting down `Fatal` crits), `+2` higher weapon proficiency, superior combat feats, and unlimited endurance with steel and bows/crossbows (`Reload 0`).

5. **Influence & Social Intrigue (Boardrooms, Courts & Underworld Networks):**
   - The domain of soft social currency (**Social Status, Respect, Favor Points & Leverage** under the **Hierarchy of Favors**), political maneuvering, guild contracts, property leases, and syndicate power. Checks rely primarily on **Diplomacy, Deception, Intimidation, Society**, and specialized lore.

## 3. Key Environmental Balance Mechanics

### A. Why Civilized Spire Society Stays Balanced (And Why Battlefield Classes Are NOT Underpowered Overall)
Because strict disarmament **only applies inside the enclosed Spire proper (Wards 1–15)**—and is **not enforced** in the Warrens, in the walled yards between the Spire proper and the Outer Wall (where workers carry axes and the wealthy carry pistols), aboard Airships (which only have the rules the Captain chooses to and can enforce), or anywhere beyond a settlement's perimeter—battlefield classes (**Clockwork Rifleman, Mad Bomber, Clockwork Engineer, Soldier, Scout**) are **never underpowered across a campaign**. They dominate 4 of the 5 operational zones while intentionally ceding the interior Spire Wards to urban specialists:
1. **The Direct-Exit Surveillance Rule (Spire Proper Only):** Inside Wards 1–15, a Soldier or Rifleman cannot simply "peace-bond" heavy armor and a greatsword/rifle and stroll around the Spire. Constables and the King's Guard stop and watch them, businesses refuse service, stealth is impossible, and ordinary citizens immediately report anyone not walking straight toward a gate.
2. **Normal-Looking Padded & Leather Only (No Clockwork Attachments in Spire Proper):** Everyday Spire armor must look like normal clothing (frock coats, dusters). Clockwork Engineers cannot wear powered exoskeletons or visible mechanical harnesses inside public wards, though **over-garments can hide material upgrades** (*Niracrest/Mitheral Weave*, *Ebon/Gildrod Leather*).
3. **Mundane Utility Dagger Cap:** While anyone can carry a daily utility knife (`1d4`) in the Spire, it **cannot** be made of rare combat materials (*Niracrest*, *Mitheral*) or have clockwork mods. This keeps the **Survivor** at **A-tier** (relying on skill and *Sneak Attack* dice) rather than out-scaling the **Pugilist** (`1d6` *Powerful Fist* + *Flurry of Blows*) or **Dandy** (legal dueling rapier).
4. **Volatile Solutions = Live Grenades in the Spire Proper:** Constables make zero distinction between manufactured bombs and improvised solutions. **Mad Bombers** are disarmed at the Spire gate (**D-tier** inside the Spire proper, **A-tier** everywhere else), and **Chirurgeons** can only carry pre-made healing items and benign precursors that *only* make healing items (**B-tier** in Spire proper, **A-tier** everywhere else).
5. **Savage Combatant Social Cost:** A **Pugilist** or **Soldier** who triggers *Savage Combatant* in a Spire brawl suffers a **–2 status penalty to all social checks (Diplomacy, Deception, Society, and Requesting Favors, except Intimidate)** during the fight and for 10 minutes afterward, making reckless fury a major liability when Constables arrive.

### B. Why Both Cartridge Gunners and Martials Shine Outside the Spire
1. **Cartridge Weapons Reward Spending Scarce Ammo:** With restored full ranges (`30–150 ft`), boosted damage (`1d8` Small / `1d10–1d12` Medium / `2d8–2d10` Large), and matching Discipline Tags waiving enhancement tier AP escalation, **Clockwork Riflemen**, **Engineers**, **Scholars**, and **Artisans** deal high, satisfying burst damage while their cartridges last.
2. **The 70% Attrition Grind & Reload Actions:** Because Small/Medium/Large cartridges require **Reload 1 / 2 / 3 actions**, cannot be recharged in the wild, and poor-quality battlefield guns have a **20% chance on every reload to overheat/burn out** (requiring a 10-minute Field Repair roll), gunners must ration shots carefully.
3. **Head-to-Head Martial Superiority:** In a stand-up fight or prolonged expedition, **Soldiers**, **Scouts**, and **Pugilists** still dominate through **35–60% more HP** (larger 3-Step Wound buffers), **higher AC & Shields** (stripping away `Fatal` crits), **`+2` Weapon Mastery**, and **action-compression combat feats**.

### C. Retainer Scaling & The Four Operating Spheres
Retainers commanded by Social Status or Influence classes (**Noble**, **Merchant**, **Grey Marketeer**) operate under codified limits to preserve party balance (`[retainers.md]`):
1. **Never Primary Defense or Offense:** Retainers were never meant to be the primary combat engine of any class. They do **not** take independent initiative turns, do **not** absorb 50% passive damage, and do **not** replace martial PCs.
2. **Two Tracks (Status vs. Class):**
   - **Social Status Track:** Base Nobles/Merchants get 1 Retainer (all levels); Greater Nobles/Wall Merchants get 1 at Lv 1, 2nd at Lv 5. Retainer Level = $\lceil \text{PC Level} / 4 \rceil$ (min 1).
   - **Influence Class Track (Noble, Merchant, Grey Marketeer):** Start with **2 Retainers** at Lv 1; gain +1 Retainer at **Levels 5, 10, 15, and 20** (max 6). Retainer Level = $\lceil \text{PC Level} / 2 \rceil$ (min 1).
3. **Four Operating Types:**
   - *Type 1 (Spire Servants & Guards):* Urban domestics/experts (`[Civilized]`) and bodyguards (short sword carry permitted for Nobles/Wall Merchants). Loyal to the House, not PC.
   - *Type 2 (The Civilized Wild):* Rural tenantry and guards outside Spire proper. Loyal to the House.
   - *Type 3 (The Warrens):* Hired toughs/guides. No house ties; expendable (no one cares if they die). Grey Marketeer hires are far more helpful/talkative, but deaths cause street backlash.
   - *Type 4 (The Outer Neither):* Hired contractors. Paid by house, but losing hires ruins reputation and forces hiring **lower-level** replacements.
4. **The Reality of Pay & Suicidal Limits (Types 2–4):** Outside the high Spire, retainers work strictly for pay. If an assignment looks suicidal, they will refuse or simply walk away without collecting pay.
5. **Part-Time PC Retainer Option:** 1 Retainer slot can be expended to grant **two (2) other PCs** part-time employment in the house: free mundane gear upgrade to base retainer level, livery, sword-carry rights (if guards have them), and hassle-free transit. Checked-out extra gear is returned at shift end. Subject to dual accountability (punished twice for crimes).

### D. Solo 1v1 Gladiator vs. Party-Wide Tactical Role
Combat ratings reflect real-world campaign utility rather than pure 1v1 arena math:
1. **The Dedicated Support Duo (Scholar & Chirurgeon):**
   - **Scholar (Complex Decision-Maker):** High-skill-ceiling tactical conductor. Constant micro-decisions balancing personal AP spend (+1/+2/+3 self bonuses, Arc Deflection reactions) against broad party telemetry broadcasts and Hardness bypass.
   - **Chirurgeon (Straightforward Lifeline):** Reliable, non-AP operational support. Pure caster chassis using INT for AC, deploying micro-drone swarms for 0-AP healing, instant bleed sealing, squad-wide Prophylactic DR, and stripping enemy Hardness via save DCs.
2. **Technical & Gear Anchors (Clockwork Engineer):** Absorbing Artisan field conditioning, the Engineer manages 10-minute field maintenance, material hardening, and heavy cartridge firearm reliability across the 70% attrition grind.
