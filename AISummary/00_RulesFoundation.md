# Clockwork Cities — Rules Foundation

## Base System
- **Pathfinder Second Edition Remaster** (ORC License).
- Core books assumed owned: *Player Core*, *Player Core 2*, *GM Core*, *Monster Core* (add others as adopted).
- These documents **do not reproduce** Pathfinder text. They reference the source and record only **changes** and **new material**.

## The Foundational Principle: Combat vs. Narrative Rules
- **Non-Combat Rules Are Descriptive, Not Prescriptive:**
  - Setting lore, social structures, ward guidelines, legal jurisdictions, institutional hierarchies, and travel interactions outside of combat are **descriptive tools designed to build the world, establish atmosphere, and provide rich context for stories**.
  - They are **not rigid legalistic straightjackets**. They guide how NPCs react and outline the stakes, but are never meant to be micromanaged like a tax code or force players into bureaucratic paralysis.
- **Combat Rules Are Exact & Prescriptive:**
  - **Only in combat are we concerned with exact mechanical precision, die rolls, action costs, and tactical math.** 
  - Combat requires rigorous, fair balance: dice rolls, AC, saving throws, damage steps, wound thresholds, ranges, cover, and action economy must function with exact, predictable clarity so players and Storytellers share an unambiguous understanding of danger and survival.
- **The Golden Table Rule:** Treat everything outside of combat as narrative clay to mold exciting roleplay; treat combat as the precise tactical engine where life, limb, and resources are put on the line.

## Precedence & The 3-Tier Design Decision Structure
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SPIRE WEAPON LAWS — ABSOLUTE CANON MANDATE:**
> Inside the Civil Spire wards, weapons are strictly governed by two universal rules:
> 1. **Universal Civilian Rule:** Only **Fists (unarmed strikes)** and **1d4 mundane utility knives/daggers** are permitted. (Even then, commoners avoid drawing knives out of fear of "Carrying with Intent" prosecution).
> 2. **The Single Exception:** **Wall Merchants, Nobles, Great Nobles, and their personal guards/retainers** are legally permitted to carry a **1d6 dueling blade** (rapier, sabre, or shortsword).
>    - **Strict Material & Modification Limit:** **No enhancements of any kind**—no exotic materials (*Niracrest*, *Mitheral*), no magic runes, and **no clockwork abilities/modifications**. Any modification automatically classifies the weapon as an illegal weapon of war, stripping the owner of their legal exemption and triggering immediate King's Guard intervention.
>    - **NO OTHER EXCEPTIONS:** Class feats, traits, concealed holsters, disguised canes, or alternative weapons (clubs, truncheons, wrenches, cutlasses, sledges) are strictly prohibited in Civil Spire wards.

1. **Specific Overrides General:**
   - Specific Clockwork rules (a character ability, location, item, or world event) override general Clockwork rules.
   - Clockwork rules override Pathfinder rules.
   - Anything not addressed uses Pathfinder Remaster as written.
2. **The 3-Tier Rules & Storytelling Decision Hierarchy:**
   Whenever designing, interpreting, or adjudicating rules at the table, decisions follow a strict three-tier priority order:
   - **Priority 1 (Highest) — Fun & Beneficial to the Game:** *Is it fun and beneficial to the game?* If not, why are we doing it this way? Enjoyment and positive table impact always come first.
   - **Priority 2 — Theme & Mood of the World:** *Does it fit the theme and mood of the Clockwork Cities world?* Crucial for atmosphere, though always secondary to Priority 1.
   - **Priority 3 (Lowest) — Realism & Rules Alignment:** *Is it realistic and does it align symmetrically with other rules in the game?* Ideally yes, but **realism and strict rules symmetry can and should be ignored whenever Priorities 1 and 2 are being met**.
3. **Storyteller Authority & Institutional Roleplay:**
   - **Members, Not Rulers (Until High Level):** Player characters are **members** of a Family House, Trade Guild, or institution—until high levels, they **do not run them**.
   - **Roleplay & Storyteller Final Word:** Questions of what an organization will fund, requisition, or require are resolved through **roleplay between the player and the DM / Storyteller**, and **it is the Storyteller—not the player—who has the final word** on what is and is not approved.
   - **The Social Contract of the Table:** Players are always free not to play or to run their own game if they dislike the Storyteller's decisions; equally, **Storytellers have the responsibility to keep the players engaged and interested enough in the game** to want to be at the table.

## Resource Limits, Storyteller Solutions & The Narrative Contract
- **Limits Create Tension, Not Playability Roadblocks:** Resource restrictions (finite Neither cartridges, 5-reload Reaction Manifold reservoirs, chemical casing needs, and food shifts) are designed to force **tactical tension and meaningful choices** within an adventure arc—they are **never intended to make classes unplayable or useless during long-term campaigns**.
- **Reasonable Storytellers Supply Solutions:** If an adventure takes the party away from civil Spires for weeks or months into unknown territory, **reasonable Storytellers will naturally provide in-world replenishment solutions**:
  - A friendly frontier outpost or isolated trading camp.
  - An abandoned clockwork workshop or ruined alchemy facility with salvageable reagents.
  - A heavily fortified Dwarven deep-mine, independent homestead, or cooperative settlement with field generators.
- **Storyteller Discretion on Availability:** These wilderness rest points may carry reasonable limits on *what* is available (e.g. they might only have sulfur salts for Fire/Acid, or limited battery charging, rather than the entire Spire catalog), but **players are never left completely high and dry**.
- **The Core Rule for All Game Limits:** This principle applies universally across Clockwork Cities: **whenever a written mechanical limitation threatens to interfere with fun or shut down character viability, the Storyteller supplies an in-world solution.** Documenting hazards and risks is essential for setting the stakes, but rules must never degenerate into "well, if this happens, your character is permanently broken and useless."

## Character Creation Philosophy — Reasons, Not Gatekeeping Justifications
Player characters are inherently exceptional, unique individuals in the world. 
- **No Gatekeeping "Justification":** The rules never require players to argue or "justify" why they should be allowed to pick an Uncommon class for their social tier, a rare archetype, or an unusual skill/Understanding. Requiring high-effort justification penalizes players for trying creative concepts and is explicitly rejected.
- **A Simple Personal Reason is Sufficient:** All that is required is a simple, believable personal reason or backstory anecdote explaining how they picked it up (e.g., *"My uncle used to smuggle deliveries before he was banished, and he would take me along to back-alley underworld bars; Mom and Dad never knew"* or *"I'm the first Survivor who spent years working in fungal farms because my family ran an illicit still behind the composting tanks"*). More detail is welcome, but a single sentence is completely sufficient.


## Social Class vs. Character Class: Complete Independence (The Singular Exception Rule)
In Clockwork Cities, **Social Class (hereditary standing, wealth, status) and Character Class (skills, training, combat discipline) are fundamentally independent systems**:
- **Any Social Class Can Play Any Character Class:** There is **zero rule** preventing a Great Noble, Middle Noble, or Merchant from being a **Survivor**, **Soldier**, **Pugilist**, **Scout**, **Artisan**, or any other class. Players are completely free to imagine and roleplay why their character has that background:
  - *Example:* A Great Noble third-son who enlisted in the royal reconnaissance corps as an elite infiltration operative (**Noble Survivor**).
  - *Example:* A Wall Merchant daughter who trained at a military academy as an expert frontline officer (**Merchant Soldier**).
  - *Example:* A middle noble disgraced or fascinated by back-alley fighting who became a bare-knuckle champion (**Noble Pugilist**).
- **The Only Restriction (The Noble & Merchant Class Prerequisite):**
  - To play the specific character classes named **Noble** or **Merchant**, you **must start with the matching Social Class** (Status 4+ for Noble; Status 3+ for Merchant; Status 6 for Wall Merchant) because their primary class features represent direct institutional governance, legal estates, commercial cartels, and guild voting rights.
  - If a Noble or Merchant character fails socially or financially and loses their title/holdings, they lose access to those high-society class perks.
- **Independence Everywhere Else:** With that single directional exception, Social Class and Character Class are completely unrelated. Having a high Social Class does not restrict you to social classes, nor does being a martial or operative class bar you from high nobility.

## Spire Social Play & How Social Skills (`Understanding`) Work
Spire play is intended to be predominantly social—the arena where roleplay-centric players shine—so Social Skills (`Understanding`) operate differently from standard mechanical tests (see [`ClockworkPathfinder/Rules/Skills.md`](file:///c:/Users/mncoc/OneDrive/Documents/Git/ClockworkCities_Pathfinder/ClockworkPathfinder/Rules/Skills.md)):
- **7 Victorian 1860 Steampunk Institutional Groups & 4-Tier Specificity Spectrum:** Players choose Broad social skills from **1. Bureaucracy & University Connections**, **2. Labor Organizations**, **3. Trade Guilds & Commercial Houses**, **4. Military**, **5. Law Enforcement**, **6. Criminal Underworld**, and **7. Nobility & High Society**, then set their narrow aspect: **Broad Only (`+0`)**, **Singular Sub-Group (`+1`)**, **Specific Place (`+2`)**, or **Specific Person (`+3`)** (more specific = greater direct impact; less specific = wider range of activities). **Intentionally Open-Ended:** The listed options are examples, not a closed list—players are always free to propose new social skills/contacts and explain why they fit a category, and should never be limited by preconceived notions so long as the group could reasonably exist and be interacted with in the setting.
- **Independent Social Advancement Track:** Separate from PF2e skill increases/feats, characters earn **Social Advancements** (`13 / 10 / 6 / 5` across Levels 1–20 by Class Social Tier) to either **add a new social skill at `Trained`** or **deepen an existing one** (`Trained [+2]` $\rightarrow$ `Expert [+4]` $\rightarrow$ `Master [+6]` $\rightarrow$ `Legendary [+8]`), with faster rates and earlier level gates favoring social classes.
- **The Storyteller's 4 Evaluation Factors & Roll-Free Specific Play:** Whenever a player uses a social skill, the Storyteller evaluates **(1) the player's standing d20 bonus**, **(2) how the player created the specific contact**, **(3) what they are asking and how well it aligns with the contact's nature**, and **(4) the risk fulfilling it causes to the person they are talking to**. **Often no roll is needed at all** when working with the **specific aspect** of a skill on an aligned, acceptable-risk request—only when the Storyteller feels **the outcome is unclear** (such as high personal risk to the contact, an out-of-character ask, or operating on the unspecific Broad level with strangers) will the Storyteller **set a Target Number (DC) and ask for a d20 roll**.
- **Cross-Border Broad Skills (`Feydial`, `Derdore` & Europe):** Because neighboring kingdoms (**Feydial** and **Derdore**) and European Spire kingdoms share Arclean's general social structure, **Broad skills still function abroad**: cross-border networks like **merchant trade routes are nearly unaffected**, while most other Broad skills suffer a **`-4` to `-8` penalty** in neighboring kingdoms (shifting to **`-4` to `-12`** in farther European kingdoms, reserved for future supplements). If a campaign spends extended time in a foreign kingdom, players can spend level-up **Social Advancements** to gain new social skills native to that location.
- **Understanding vs. Obligation & The Inquisitive Ripple:**
  - **`Understanding` (Social Skills):** Supplies operational intelligence about organizations and groups—what they are routinely willing to do (both legal and illicit), who their key members are, and safe approaches. It provides the framework players need to formulate actionable plans.
  - **`Obligation` (`Tier 1 Trivial` to `Tier 5 Life Debt`):** A limited-use debt that directly *compels* behavior from a specific person or organization. Requires no roll; the target must comply or suffer severe personal and organizational **Social Standing loss**.
  - **The Inquisitive Ripple:** Occurs **only** when a request or investigation requires a d20 roll (e.g., reaching beyond routine tasks, asking for high-risk favors, or pushing contacts). The Storyteller always provides an explicit upfront warning that the player is pushing their contacts and people might notice before the dice are cast.

## Spire Combat Reality: Rarity, Escalation & The Third-Party Law Enforcement Influx
While wilderness and Neither expeditions feature prolonged attrition, **open combat within the Spire (PC vs. Anything Else) is exceptionally rare and fundamentally asymmetrical**:
- **Not a Closed Arena (Never 1-Group vs. 1-Group):** Combat in the Spire virtually *never* remains an isolated skirmish between the PCs and an opposing faction. Spires are dense, interconnected urban organisms filled with watchful residents, whistle-blowing citizens, and tight patrol beats.
- **Immediate Escalation & Alert Radius:** Any physical altercation, drawn blade, or chemical blast immediately alerts bystanders. Within 1 to 3 rounds, **Ward Constables** converge on the scene. If combat involves military ordnance, Neither cartridges, or high-status nobility, **The King's Guard** is summoned.
- **The True Objective (Disengage or Control):** Prolonged gunfights or multi-round brawls are suicidal inside the walls. Combatants must either end the conflict instantly in round 1, contain the scene behind closed doors with zero noise, or disengage and vanish before law enforcement cordons off the ward block.

## Combat Encounter Philosophy & Threat Distribution (The 15 / 70 / 15 Rule)
Combat encounters in Clockwork Cities are designed across three distinct gameplay bands:
- **15% — "We Are Heroes" (Showcase / Low Risk):** Players face lower-level adversaries (slum thugs, lone sentries, disorganized goblins). Little to no risk; allows players to show off tactical supremacy and class abilities while validating their progression without burning vital resources.
- **70% — "The Grind" (Grueling Attrition & Meaningful Peril):** Coordinated patrols, fortified positions, orc raiders, and wild Neither beasts. Resources (cartridges, auto-injectors, shield HP) are heavily burned; players genuinely sweat over survival, and tactical missteps cause characters to drop into **Critical (days to recover)** or **Mortal (weeks to recover)** wound thresholds.
- **15% — "Boss Fights / The Crucible" (Mortal Lethality):** Apex mutated behemoths, Clockwork Colossi, deep-zone monstrosities, or royal inquisitor strike teams. Long-term trauma, limb destruction, and permanent PC death are real possibilities if players do not plan, scout, and execute flawless teamwork.
- **Realistic Animal Lethality & Mega-Fauna Scaling:** Standard Pathfinder undertones large predators. In Clockwork Cities, physics and mass apply: an unmutated **Grizzly Bear is a Level 7 Boss (requiring 4–5 Level 5 PCs working in unison, or one Level 10 martial solo)**.
- **The 50% Disengagement Rule & Storyteller Safety Valve:**
  - *Self-Preservation:* Natural beasts do not fight to the death; taking 50% HP damage causes them to search for escape and disengage. Only cornered or raging beasts fight to the death.
  - *The Real Storyteller Out:* When an encounter becomes unexpectedly lethal or drags, the Storyteller simply has the creature flee—ignoring cornered/raging status and treating HP as spent behind the screen.
  - *The "Act Like You Planned It" Rule:* When players use biological logic (e.g. popping a flare against lightless mine beasts to cause -2 attacks), the Storyteller immediately treats it as an intentional in-world weakness, rewarding creative thought over raw dice rolling.

## Adversary Architecture: PC Classes & Creature Templates (Levels 1–15)
Adversaries across Levels 1–15 are built rapidly without designing custom monsters from scratch:
- **Humanoids:** Built directly using **PC Classes** (Soldiers, Pugilists, Scouts, Officers, Mad Bombers, Chirurgeons).
- **Fauna & Aberrants:** Built by taking unmodified baseline Pathfinder animals and adding 1 to 10 levels of **Modular Creature Templates** (`ClockworkPathfinder/Classes/NPC/creature class/`):
  - **1. Brute (STR):** Muscle hypertrophy, flat damage, knockback, physical resistance, +5 to +50 ft speed.
  - **2. Stalker (DEX):** AC, Reflex, +10 to +50 ft speed, stealth, pounce, precision & bleed.
  - **3. Juggernaut (CON):** Massive HP, physical resistance, Fast Healing 2–12, Trample, stun immunity.
  - **4. Venomous (Poison):** Injected bites/stings, blinding spurts, skunk-style musk spray cones.
  - **5. Biochemical (Acid/Fire):** Caustic phlegm spit, acid splash, vomit cones, death detonation bursts.
  - **6. Galvanic (Electricity):** Dielectric scales, retaliatory shock vs metal weapons, lightning absorption.
  - **7. Pack (Coordination):** Flanking precision, synchronized tripping/takedowns, harrying circles.
  - **8. Burrower (Subterranean):** Burrow speeds, tremorsense, sinkholes, drag-under suffocation.
  - **Incomplete Adaptation:** Rapid de-buffing template (step-down damage dice, -2 stats, flaws) for maladapted or displaced creatures.
- **Rule of Thumb:** All template modifications are optional; erring on the side of making a creature slightly too weak is never an issue, but stacking heavy buffs requires adjusting the effective Level (CR) upward.

## Mitigating Character Death — The Institutional Lineage Succession Rule
Because Clockwork Cities features deep institutional backing (*"Worth = who you represent"*) alongside high mortal combat stakes, player deaths do not force players to abandon what they have worked for:
- **Encouraged (Not Mandatory) Continuity:** Players are encouraged to create their replacement character within the same **Family House, Trade Guild, Military Regiment, or Syndicate** (e.g., a junior apprentice, sibling officer, or branch cousin).
- **Inherited Assets:** Successor characters within the same institution inherit **Permanent Wealth** (housing, workshops, lab benches, guild credit), **House-issued gear** (re-issued without cost), accumulated **Faction Standing** (starting at old Standing –10), and up to **half of the deceased character's social contacts and Focus Understandings** (passed down via ledgers, diaries, or family records).
- **Freedom to Pivot:** Players who prefer an entirely clean break can roll a new character from any other social tier at standard party level and baseline starting gear, leaving their previous house behind as potential allies or rivals.

## Magic & Spellcasting Classes — Removed & Replaced
- **Supernatural Magic Removed:** True supernatural magic does not exist in the Clockwork Cities setting. Rather than retaining stripped-down caster classes with artificial level caps or multiclass-only restrictions, all traditional spellcasting classes (Wizard, Cleric, Druid, Witch, Bard, Sorcerer, Oracle) have been **completely removed from character creation**.
- **Role Redistribution into Steampunk Classes:** All essential utility, tactical, buffing, and support roles previously performed by magical classes have been permanently converted into physical, non-magical steampunk classes:
  - **Cleric & Divine Restoration:** Removed. Replaced by the **Chirurgeon** (cellular-level restorative sera, biological surgeries, antitoxins, and sterile medicines).
  - **Wizard & Elemental Blasting:** Removed. Replaced by the **Mad Bomber** (chemical, concussive, incendiary, and aerosolized gas grenades) and the **Clockwork Engineer** (arc emitters, kinetic shockwave engines, and electrical capacitors).
  - **Druid & Primal Survival:** Removed. Replaced by the **Scout** (covering both surface Neither fog survival/outriding and subterranean excavation, tremorsense, and mine sapping).
  - **Bard & Performance Buffs:** Removed. Replaced by the **Merchant** (commercial directives, tactical financial incentives, contract enforcement, and accelerated Rogue skill mastery).
  - **Witch / Occult & Debuffs:** Removed. Replaced by the **Inspector** (forensic deduction, criminology, and evidence analysis) and the **Chirurgeon** (caustic acids, debilitating toxins, and neurochemical irritants).
- **Spells as Physical Deliverables Only:** Spells do not exist as verbal incantations, hand gestures, or mystical focus pools. Any spell-like effect that exists in the setting is an explicitly physical **Clockwork / Neither-Tech Device** (pneumatic launchers, breather tanks, arc discharge projectors, pulse capacitors) or biomedical **Concoction** (cellular stimulants, antitoxins, aerosol canisters) per [`AISummary/10_Magic.md`](file:///c:/Users/mncoc/OneDrive/Documents/Git/ClockworkCities_Pathfinder/AISummary/10_Magic.md):
  - **Environmental Constraints:** Neither-tech operates at 100% in Neither, 1/2 damage/duration in the Zone, and is completely inoperable in pure Aither.
  - **Energy Distribution:** Great Spires broadcast wireless radiant power shared across the Neither by tapping Zone squalls; subterranean Dwarven/Deep One settlements use local dynamos. Portable energy relies on rechargeable Civilian cells (~1/20th density) or Spire-synthesized High-Density Military Cartridges powered by an unstable biochemical catalyst.
  - **Manufactured Science vs. Improvised Field Alchemy:** High-stability Neither creations (weapon cartridges, dynamos, long-shelf-life sera) require institutional cleanrooms and coordinated teams of professional **Artisans** (machining containment) and **Scholars** (chemical stoichiometry). Conversely, **Chirurgeons** and **Mad Bombers** excel at intuitive field improvisation, adapting dirty, scavenged reagents on the fly to stabilize temporary minor concoctions and field explosives during daily preparation.
  - **Instability & Slots:** Without continuous tuning or stabilization by a trained "caster", primed field devices and concoctions detonate, spoil, or discharge into inert scrap. Daily slots represent the active threshold of unstable items an engineer or alchemist can calibrate and maintain.
  - **Dual-System NeitherTeck Economy:** Gear runs on a clean two-part resource system replacing magical attunement: **Passive Loadout Capacity** ($\text{Key Stat Mod} + \lfloor \tfrac{\text{Level}}{2} \rfloor + 4$ across 3 class tracks, with armor weight discounts/penalties) sets continuous protective/environmental equipment during daily prep; **Active Surge AP** ($\text{INT Mod} + \text{Base} + \text{Scaling}$) is spent per-action on combat triggers and strikes.
  - **The Level 12 Retail Store Ceiling:** Spire commerce offers retail gear up to **Level 12** (`+2 Greater Striking / +2 Greater Resilient`). Levels 13–20 gear consists of **Heroic Relics, Era 1 Vault technology, and custom masterworks** acquired exclusively outside the store system via expeditions, rare monster harvests (abyssal leviathans), and royal artisan commissions.
