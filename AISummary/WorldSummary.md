## 1. CORE PREMISE
- Genre: steampunk-ish politics/intrigue + light brawling. Tone: status > individual. "Worth = who you represent" (house/guild/family). Individuals untrusted; group backing is everything.
- Default play: residents of **Ward 13 (Luxury Ward)** of **New Avalon** (capital spire of Arclean). Ward 13 is the **great social nexus** where classes mingle: Nobles and Greater Nobles descend to dine, spoiled aristocratic heirs drink and gamble, and Wall Merchants consult master crafters or prepare for Warrens visits. It is the absolute highest ward that well-off citizens (without vast wealth) can travel to without a personal invitation or permit (partial functional exceptions exist for the University and Ward 15 Sky Docks, where all classes have operational reasons to visit). Low Circle features prized exterior parks; Warrens behind Ward 13 is a semi-legal luxury gambling/club underworld run by **Johnny Ralen** and **Amanda Coren**—ruthless, highly polished business magnates providing high-stakes gaming dens, discreet salons, and decadent entertainment for nobles and Wall Merchants seeking a weekend escape from restrictive Spire decorum. Just like Vegas, options exist for anyone with spare coin willing to part with it: ordinary citizens residing in Ward 13 can comfortably afford a night or two on the town each month, and influential figures rising from the lower wards have ample wealth to indulge. Crossing or offending either Ralen or Coren carries consequences every bit as dangerous as crossing a Greater Noble or the matriarch/patriarch of a Wall Merchant house. Their houses rarely resort to crude violence or breaking kneecaps; instead, they wield the double threat of legal collection through the Spire courts paired with social ruin and public contempt that destroys a debtor's standing and credit across the city. The King's Guard maintains no garrison office here but is frequently seen on roving patrols, and the houses employ their own private constables and security enforcers who are noticeably far less brutal than constabulary forces in the slum wards—focused on professional hospitality, de-escalation, and encouraging repeat business rather than heavy-handed displays of power over patrons.
- Physics: Combustion unreliable in Neither: fuel releases normal energy, but 5%–90% is absorbed into the Neither and returned randomly back to the location within 3 minutes → causes sudden dying out or 5–6× explosive surges; makes cars, black powder guns, standard internal combustion non-functional. Clockwork steam engines use heavy mechanical flywheels and pressure dampeners to smooth out energy spikes. Neither also drives ongoing mutation until creatures adapt to their density; substances/biochemicals formed in Neither decay in seconds to days when exposed to sunlight/Aither (Neithercrete degrades over months).
- **Radiant Power Grid & Infrastructure [CANON]:**
  - **Shared Radiant Power:** Within Great Spires, Dwarven subterranean cities, and Deep One layers, energy is distributed wirelessly through the Neither medium via **radiant power**, supplied by massive, heavily guarded municipal generators and catalytic dynamos.
  - **The Spire Advantage (Zone Interception):** The **Great Spires that physically cross and pierce the Zone** are the **only places on the planet** where enough raw electrical energy is constantly harvested to broadcast radiant power across vast urban districts (powering thousands of wireless ~60W street lamps, industrial lift winches, and municipal machinery without physical power lines).
  - **Dwarven & Deep One Grids:** Subterranean cities rely on deep geothermal taps, mineral catalytic reactors, or captured Neither pockets to run their guarded generators, but their radiant grids cover much tighter, contained subterranean perimeters compared to the sprawling vertical spires.

## 2. ATMOSPHERIC LAYERS [CANON]
Standard terms (src normalized): **Aither** = clean air; **Neither** = corrupting medium; **Zone**; **Neithercrete** (spire material; old alias Natherium).
| Layer | Desc |
|---|---|
| Aither | clean air above Zone; rulers/nobles/king live here |
| Zone ("dead zone") | 20–150 ft (elsewhere 100–300 ft, +200 in storms) Aither/Neither mix; constant lightning storms; powers airships & cities. Crossing: 1) Within spires/grounded structures; 2) Dual-purpose sea/airships (simple waterproofing, but sails/oars take cargo space); 3) Land tunnels (3/4+ mi long, heavily guarded at ends where Zone touches mountains/north); 4) Zone Running (gamble: calm/fast terrain = 1 in 20 death; rough/slow terrain = up to 15 in 20 death; storms = up to 100% death; even L20 fully geared dies from 1 strike). |
| Upper/Light Neither | sun & moon visible; humans' natural home |
| Lower/Dark Neither | sun not locatable; most planet surface; orcs' home |
| Lightless Neither / Underground | caves, deep valleys; dwarves |
| The Deep / Mist | 5,000–12,000 ft underground, thick; deep ones |
- **Why The Zone? (Airship Design & Tactical Rationale):**
  - **Power Source & Ceiling:** Airships require radiant energy harvested directly from the electrical squalls of the Zone to power their propulsion engines. Flying significantly above or below the Zone causes engines to starve and stall.
  - **No "Bomb from Above" Solution:** The extreme turbulence, updrafts, and lightning in the Zone make precision high-altitude carpet-bombing impossible. You cannot simply sit safely in the clouds and obliterate surface fortifications or spires below.
  - **Collapses 3D Flight into 2D Tactical Naval Combat:** The atmospheric density and electromagnetic equilibrium of the Zone create a uniform buoyancy plane: **all vessels naturally float at the exact same altitude/level in the same location**. Vertical displacement is minimal, and vertical "waves" (thermal cloud swells) over long distances are far gentler than ocean swells. Engagements function strictly as **2-dimensional naval combat** on a flat sea of clouds, featuring broadsides, boarding actions, and ramming tactics—mirroring age-of-sail naval warfare with enhanced maneuverability due to radiant clockwork propulsion rather than wind/steam sails.
- Neither Fog: periodic dense surges, lethal; reason humans live only in big settlements/caravans.
- Neither corrupts people/plants. Neither fungi feed off it, rot in any sunlight/electric light (harvested under special photosensitive light).
- Permanent structure reduces environmental exposure by 1 level. Hermetically sealed deep spires reduce exposure by 2 levels.
- Neither Storm Mitigation: Storms lose intensity as they penetrate below ground or through structures: –1 intensity per 100 ft depth in mines (at 300+ ft deep, storms have zero mechanical effect, only an audible howl); –1 for reinforced structures (spires/hamlets); –1 for native Orc physiology.
- Neither cycles ~5,000 yrs (full cover ↔ absent). Now ~1/6 down from an unusually strong peak.

## 3. HUMANITY: 6 TYPES (one species, environment-driven) [CANON]
Not separate species. Shifts ~1 type per ~3 gens of living in non-native layer (range 1–10+). Statistical visual overlap: ~20% of Half-Elves look like 20% of Humans, and ~20% of Humans look like 20% of Orcs. While large groups (50–100 individuals) are easily identified by population averages (e.g. an Orc raiding warband vs. an Elven retinue), identifying individuals in isolation is extremely difficult. Using a Disguise Kit allows 100% of Half-Elves and Orcs to pass as Human on a successful Deception check. **Dominance by Shift Limits:** Humans and Orcs dominate civilization (~98% global pop) because their central position caps their maximum shift at **3 (Total Shift 6.0)**, surviving travel anywhere. Half-Elves and Dwarves have max shift **4 (Total Shift 8.0)**, keeping them in specialized niches. High Elves and Deep Ones have max shift **5 (Total Shift 10.0)**, suffering rapid lethal necrotic shock outside native sanctuaries and rendering them virtually unplayable abroad without extreme life support. Interbreeding: 1 shift viable (80% mother's type), 2 shifts rare, 3 essentially never. Child ≤1 shift from mother; parents must be ≤2 shifts apart (High Elf+Human ok, High Elf+Orc no). Same 6-form pattern applies to most other life.

## 4. EXPOSURE, FOOD & RECOVERY [CANON concept, PF modernized in ClockworkPathfinder/Rules/TheNeither.md]
- Environmental distance is calculated via **Total Shift Score = Air Shift Levels (integer steps 0 to 5) + Food Shift Levels (0.5-point increments for mixed diets)**.
- **Combined Total Shift Healing Table:**
  - **Score 0.0 to 1.5:** **100% Normal Healing Rate.** No rest or recovery penalties.
  - **Score 2.0 to 3.5:** **50% Normal Healing Rate.** HP rest recovery takes twice as long.
  - **Score 4.0 to 5.0:** **25% Normal Healing Rate.** Sluggish recovery; 1/4 HP restored per rest.
  - **Score 5.5 to 6.0:** **No Natural Healing + (1d8 + 5) Damage per Day** (begins 24 hours after exposure; cellular necrosis).
  - **Score 6.5 or Higher:** **No Natural Healing + (3d8 + 10) Damage per Day** (begins 24 hours after exposure; acute systemic collapse, rapid death).
  - **Starvation / Food Refusal:** Refusing to eat foreign food counts as a **+0.5 Food Shift accumulation per shift/day** of fasting.
  - **Convalescence:** Requires a 7-day stabilization period in clean environment eating native food (or deep-water fish), followed by **3 points healed per week** of clean living.
- **Air Exposure (Physical Decay — Clumsy, Enfeebled, Drained):**
  - Level 0: Equilibrium (0).
  - Level 1: Not Optimal (–1 after 1 month; does not worsen).
  - Level 2: Severe Strain (–2; accumulates every 1 week).
  - Level 3: Cellular Shock (–4; accumulates every 3 days).
  - Level 4: Tissue Necrosis (–8; accumulates every 1 day).
  - Level 5: Void Shock (–16; accumulates every 1 hour; fatal within hours).
- **Food Exposure (Cognitive & Social Decay — Stupefied, –Int/Cha Checks & DCs):**
  - Level 0: Equilibrium (0).
  - Level 1: Not Optimal (–1 after 1 month; does not worsen).
  - Level 2: Moderate Shift (–2; accumulates every 1 month).
  - Level 3: Severe Shift (–4; accumulates every 1 week).
  - Level 4: Toxic Poisoning (–8; accumulates every 1 day).
  - Level 5: Catastrophic Shock (–16; accumulates every 1 hour; fatal convulsions).
- **The Deep Water Fish Exception:** Types 1 to 4 (High Elf, Half-Elf, Human, Orc) can all eat deep saltwater fish (150+ ft) or deep freshwater fish as neutral native food (**0 Food Shift**).
- **28-Day Cumulative Exposure Window:** Shift-workers (miners, foundriers) logging 12-hr shifts calculate exposure over a rolling 28-day window; part-time labor accumulates conditions over weeks without triggering lethal healing collapse if given clean food.
- **Unified Recovery Rule:** 1-week continuous stabilization delay in native environment/diet, followed by **–1 point healed per week** for both Air and Food.
- Staple: **Sycelium** mushroom, grows fast in dark; 10×10×10 ft feeds 15 raw / 10 processed. **Mela** = processed Sycelium, safest civilized staple.
- Mitigation: Architecture reduces Air Shift by 1 level; hermetic deep vaults reduce by 2; respirators reduce by 1 (8 hrs); every 100 ft mine depth reduces storm intensity by 1 level (300+ ft = 0 damage). Medical: Mela Cleanse resets Food Shift in 7 days; Hyperbaric Aither sanatorium clears 1 Air Shift per 72 hrs.

- **Elven Tech, Combustion Engines & Gunpowder (The Elven Mystique & Geographic Defense):** 
  - Elves maintain a limited supply of circa-1900 technologies: combustion-engine trains, motorcars, and rifled gunpowder firearms (note: isolated elven populations may lack access to these advanced stockpiles).
  - **Combustion Engine Failure Outside Aither:** Any standard internal combustion engine suffers the exact same atmospheric failure as black powder: inside the Neither or Zone, combustion is erratic, causing engines to misfire, stall, or suffer catastrophic fuel explosions.
  - **Restricted Access & Geographic Inaccessibility:** Visiting any elven settlement **requires traveling directly through the perilous lightning storms of the Zone**. 
    - Only a few rare locations have airship docks that directly reach land.
    - Reaching elven territories otherwise demands traversing through **long, heavily fortified subterranean tunnels** bored beneath the Zone, or utilizing **specialized amphibious airships capable of landing and transforming into ocean-sailing vessels** upon the northern seas.
  - **Geopolitical Security (Why Elves Fear No Invasion):** Between their lethal technological superiority in the Aither (train networks, motor transport, rapid artillery) and extremely restricted, easily choked travel chokepoints (Zone squalls, defended tunnels, and Arctic sea approaches), the Elves have zero fear of military invasion despite their small global population (~300k).
  - **Gunpowder Hazards:** Outside the Aither, firing black powder requires a d10 check (1–3 squib; 4–6 normal; 7–10 explodes). Powder travels the Zone safely only in sealed insulated cases; in the Neither, all gunpowder has a 20%/day chance of spontaneous detonation (`ClockworkPathfinder/Rules/RangedWeapons.md`).

## 5. HISTORY (timeline) [CANON, partly ?]

1. ≤1715 = real history. 1715 Neither appears; 1730 combustion instability; 1740 Zone forms at equator; 1810 tropics covered; 1890 Deep Neither; 1910 equator impassable.
2. First era: **7 Great Kingdoms** (English, German, Scandinavian, Western American, China, India). Wealthy live in Aither/north/peaks; working masses forced into Neither; first Neither-tech (early Neithercrete, radiant lights, stationary arc weapons).
3. First Age of Storms (2110–2400): total cover; Zone travels past Iceland; human speciation (Humans under Zone, Orcs in Deep Neither by 2400, Dwarves underground ~2600, Deep Ones in boiling depths, Elves in untouched Aither). Fungi (Sycelium) mutate to feed on Neither.
4. First Age of Proto-Spires (~2400–3900): Zone drops; 500-ft proto-spires; global tech drops to ~1580 level; orc hordes resist; ~50 regional kingdoms; 1,500 static years of siege/loss of ancient knowledge.
5. Second Great Storm (~3900–4100): Zone sweeps past Iceland; Dwarven subterranean citadels rise; orc hordes sweep south and destroy dwarven cities; Iceland survives in isolation.
6. ~1500 yrs ago: Neither drops; Icelanders & Dwarves improve Neithercrete (500 ft → miles-high). **Age of Spires** (~200 yrs of building).
7. Third Great Storm + **fungal collapse** (~1100–900 ya): fungi die off; ~80% of spire humanity starves over 80 yrs. Dwarves & Deep Ones breed hundreds of resilient fungal strains (preventing repeat).

8. ~700 yrs ago: **Airships** (ride the Zone, ~100 mi/day) → **Age of Conquest** (700–300 ya): drop troops on spire tops, starve cities; small arc cannons.
9. ~300 ya: **Great Arc Cannons** (Zone-powered; bigger = more range/dmg) → spires near-impregnable; 200+ yrs stability. Merchant houses appear (~300 ya).
10. ~100 ya: university professionals/professional guilds appear (2021 doc).
11. ~50 ya: long-range airships → new trade routes. ~10 ya: faster/longer; contact with lands months away that never saw airships.
12. **40 ya: Neither Cartridge Weapons** — Non-projectile energy weapons powered by rechargeable cartridges of captured Neither energy. Project directed electricity (arc) or kinetic force (pulse). 
    - **Environmental Constraints & Capacity:** Operate at 100% effectiveness only in the Neither; in the Zone, they deal **1/2 damage or 1/2 duration**; in pure Aither, they fail completely. Feature **tripled shot capacity** per cartridge cylinder compared to traditional single-shot firearms.
    - **The Secret Biochemical Component (Spire Monopoly):** Weapon cartridges are more than simple batteries; their high capacity relies on a **classified biochemical catalyst** that only Great Spires possess the resources and sterile laboratories to produce. Production is industrial, carried out by teams of professional **Artisans, Scholars, and factory technicians** working collaboratively in controlled environments. Once synthesized, these chemicals are **extremely unstable if directly exposed to either Neither or Aither** (detonating or dissolving if ruptured), requiring airtight insulated casings.
    - **Civil vs. Weapon Energy Cartridges:** Most Neither tech (lamps, tools, winches) runs on low-density civil cells storing **~1/20th the energy by size** of military cartridges. While 20 civil cells can theoretically recharge one weapon cell, the prohibitive bulk (~4–8 kg per reload) makes traditional bows and crossbows far cheaper and easier for wandering groups.
    - **Charging Infrastructure:** Permanent charging dynamos are massive installations operated by Spires, Dwarven citadels, Deep One enclaves, and civilized Orc clans. Mobile raiding warbands lack these facilities and cannot recharge captured weapon cells in the wild. Cartridges hold a charge for 3–6 months. Sieges viable again: outlast a defending city's cartridge reserves, then breach defenses. **Last 20 yrs**: several cities fallen; small kingdoms merging; arms race; airship armadas. ← CURRENT TENSION
13. Ruins: "Atlantis" (last low-Neither valley civ, common ruins); older peak civ (very advanced, rare strange ruins).

## 6. WHERE HUMANS LIVE & THE FRONTIER SPECTRUM [CANON]
- **The Dual Cultural & Geographic Model:**
  - **The Great Spires = Victorian London (c. 1860):** Major eastern metropolises of class hierarchy, guild charters, gas/radiant lamps, and unarmed municipal wards. **New Avalon is one of—if not the—largest and safest Spires in the world**; other Spires and settlements carry far greater risk.
  - **Moving Away from the Spires = Radiating Control Into the Wild West:** Settlements **radiate outward from each Spire**, with the closest areas being the most controlled and Spire authority fading as walking/riding distance moves from **hours to days**:
    1. *Hours of walking/riding:* Tightly controlled agricultural shires, walled hamlets, and patrolled caravan trails.
    2. *Days of walking/riding:* **Tombstone / Deadwood-style frontier & mining towns**—diminishing Spire control, rowdy boomtowns, deep-shaft company camps, frontier marshals, hired guns, and saloon shootouts.
    3. *Outside Spire control (Days to weeks out):* Like entering the **Alaskan wilderness—except neither empty nor peaceful**, and surface realities do not align with air borders. These unaligned lands contain **Orc Warband territories** (such as northern Scotland) and roaming bands, **communities of free people who refuse to swear loyalty to the King**, **Dwarven mines with surface exits** in seemingly abandoned areas, and the **remnants of older Spires and ancient Dwarven strongholds** holding forgotten history and rare materials (`Niracrest`, `Mitheral`, pre-collapse relics) that have yet to be plundered.
  - **The "Unspoken Small War" & Frontier Pragmatism [CANON]:**
    - **Surface Travel & The 6-Day Information Lag:** Beneath the Zone in the heavy Neither, distance is deceptive and travel is brutally grueling. Even on maintained carriage roads, **maintaining 20 miles a day is difficult, and pushing for 40 miles in a single day is the absolute physical limit**. 
      - Consequently, an outpost, mine, or border hamlet located just **120 miles from a Spire lives under a minimum 6-day communication turnaround** (3 days for a mounted rider or runner to reach the Spire, and 3 days for orders or relief troops to ride back).
      - **The Best-Case Baseline vs. Reality:** That 6-day turnaround represents **the absolute best of conditions** on clear roads with fresh mounts. Under adverse weather, toxic cloud-fronts, local Neither hazards, or when navigating rough country without fixed, maintained roads, **the message round trip routinely extends to well over 3 weeks**.
      - Spires cannot micromanage or instantly protect the perimeter; frontier garrisons and outposts must solve their own problems or perish long before help arrives.
    - **Perpetual Low-Intensity Border Skirmishes:** Along the porous frontier margins—between Arclean, Derdore, Feydial, and the roaming Orc warbands—**an unending, low-intensity "small war" is constantly waged**. Border forts, satellite hamlets, and deep-shaft mines **routinely change hands multiple times over the course of a few years**.
    - **Frontier Cynicism & Opportunistic Loyalty:** The rugged miners, homesteaders, and outland survivors living in these borderlands have zero romantic illusions about national pride. Caught between ravenous Orc warbands and the shifting armies of distant crowns, **they adopt a deeply pragmatic, tongue-in-cheek approach to survival**:
      - *When the frontier shifts:* *"Well, you've driven off the Orcs and reopened the road to a Derdore Spire? Guess I'm a proud Derdorean today."*
      - *When Arclean retakes the site:* *"Just saw that battle—you boys took back the guard post and re-established the caravan route to New Avalon? By the King, thank you for freeing us from the vile Derdoreans! Forcing loyal Arcleans to dig ore for a foreign crown is an absolute crime against humanity!"*
      - Outland folk say whatever is necessary to keep their families fed, their mines running, and their settlements from being burned to the ground by whichever flag holds the local garrison this season.
    - **The Middle Frontier (Between Spire Control & The Wild West) [CANON]:**
      - **Calmer, Stable Allegiance:** Situated between the tightly policed inner agricultural shires (hours away) and the lawless Wild West frontier (4+ days out), these intermediate settlements are considerably calmer. They do not switch national flags back and forth; the community knows exactly which **Great Noble House holds the royal lease**, who manages the land, and whom they work for.
      - **The Impoverished Noble Magistrate:** Local administration is typically overseen by an appointed **Mayor and Magistrate Judge** drawn from a **Lesser Noble House that has lost most of its fortunes and resources**, desperately accepting an outland posting to avoid losing their noble social standing.
      - **Frontier Accountability ("Fell Down the Same Stair 8 Times"):** Governance in this middle tier operates on a blunt, unwritten local contract: **competent, fair magistrates live long and respected lives; abusive, tyrannical magistrates mysteriously "fall down the same flight of stairs eight times in a row."** While formal royal appeal is impractical, community self-policing ensures that the local magistrate balances the demands of the lease with the survival of the folk.
      - **Settlement Culture Beyond 4 Days (The True Wild West Model):** Once a settlement, mining town, or trade hub lies **more than 4 days' travel from a Spire**, Spire civilization completely gives way to an authentic **Wild West reality**:
      - **Ubiquitous Cartridge Guns:** Cartridge firearms (rifles, pistols, carbines) are common tools of daily life. They are acquired through **salvaging battlefield skirmishes, trading with roaming mercenaries, or direct purchase from Spire merchants** bringing crates of surplus arms out to frontier posts.
      - **Military Hands-Off Policy:** Because of the "small war" mentality, national armies operating in the region know that local miners and homesteaders have **zero interest in participating in kingdom wars**. Armies do not confiscate local firearms, knowing full well that **without guns, the locals would be overrun by Orcs and Neither beasts**, which would halt the vital flow of coal, ore, and timber into the Spires. As long as locals stay out of military skirmishes, soldiers leave them armed.
      - **Recharging Hubs & Town Generators:** Larger frontier settlements and trading hubs often maintain **independent dynamos/generators and specialized workshops capable of recharging Neither cartridges**, creating bustling frontier boomtowns where prospectors, bounty hunters, and trappers resupply.
      - **Town Gun Laws (Tombstone Rules):** Depending on the local council or town marshal, individual towns enforce their own firearm statutes—some allow citizens to walk the streets openly armed, while others strictly **force travelers to turn over their guns at the gatehouse or sheriff's office** for the duration of their stay.
      - **Frontier Justice & Dead-or-Alive Bounties:** With royal magistrates days or weeks away, crime is handled almost exclusively through **local town law, armed marshals, and posted bounties on wanted outlaws**, paid out to freelancers and bounty-hunting posses.
    - **Military Border Outposts & The Martial Law Enclave [CANON]:**
      - **Spire Law Preserved in the Wilds:** No matter how deep into the untamed Neither or how many weeks' travel from a Spire an outpost is sited, **military forts and garrisons exist firmly within the legal framework and social order of the Spires**.
      - **Perpetual Martial Law:** Inside the fort's perimeter, the environment is governed by **permanent martial law**. Military discipline, rank protocol, and curfew are absolute.
      - **Civilians and Camp Followers:** Attached civilian personnel, contractors, sutlers, and wandering merchants who arrive to sell goods understand that **expected behavior, legal decorum, and compliance are virtually identical to being inside the Spire itself**. Civilian dissimulation, insubordination, or black-market agitation inside the fort is met with immediate military tribunal justice.
    - *The Bad Times & The Reality of Spire Sieges:* Those Orc warbands and bandit coalitions push inward and **attack the settled frontier—overrunning mining towns, burning hamlets, and laying siege to a Spire**. 
      - *Impregnable Defenses (The ~2/3 Rule):* About **two-thirds (2/3) of the Spires in each kingdom possess defenses nearly as massive and fortified as New Avalon itself** (colossal concentric outer/inner walls, Great Arc Cannons, and multi-tier perimeter ramparts). Barring a catastrophic betrayal, technological failure, or an overwhelming external military threat (such as an armada from a rival kingdom), **an Orcish horde cannot breach or take a major Spire by assault—even if the Spire is cut off from all external support.**
      - *The Supply Chokehold & The Misery of Isolation:* However, an Orc siege cuts the vital arteries linking the Spire to its surrounding agrarian shires, outlying mines, and satellite hamlets. Without the steady influx of grain, meat, coal, ore, and timber:
        - Life inside the Spire rapidly becomes **nearly unlivable**.
        - The population is reduced strictly to **fungal `Sycelium`/mushrooms** from the deep Warrens and whatever meager produce grows on the outdoor agricultural step-back terraces.
        - **All luxuries rapidly vanish**, high society collapses into survival rationing, and manufacturing grinds to a halt.
        - The Spire becomes wholly dependent on high-risk, exorbitantly priced **airship cargo imports**—a catastrophic fiscal trap, as the beleaguered Spire, stripped of its mines and workshops, has **virtually no manufactured trade goods left to offer in exchange**.
      - While the smaller 1/3 of frontier Spires face total physical destruction and breach, for the great Spires an Orc invasion is a slow, agonizing economic and psychological strangulation.
  - **The Sea & The Zone (1860s Trade Lanes $\rightarrow$ 1650–1730 Golden Age of Piracy):**
    - *Near Spires & Patrolled Trade Routes:* Vessels traveling in the Zone or surface waters face **1860s Victorian maritime conditions** (customs cutters, regulated convoys, naval blockades, and Spire Great Arc Cannon umbrellas).
    - *Out of Sight of Spires & Patrol Lanes:* Once beyond the sight of the Spires and off major patrolled lanes, both the seas and the 2D cloud-plane of the Zone shift into the **Golden Age of Piracy (1650–1730)**—complete with pirate coves/cloud-havens, broadside and boarding warfare, and **rival kingdoms funding privateers (*Letters of Marque*) who operate among legitimate merchants** before seizing rival vessels and cargo in open skies or waters.
    - *Open Inter-Kingdom Warfare & Sudden Spire Blockades:* Militaries from different kingdoms periodically engage in **open warfare**, and traveling crews may arrive at a destination Spire they intended to trade with only to **suddenly find it under a full naval/aerial blockade**—enforced either by **their own kingdom's military** or by a **random/rival kingdom's armada**.
- Great cities/spires 87% (¼–⅓ of each city in Warrens). Sea level up to 4+ mi tall. Many never leave birth city.
- Hamlets 7.75%: noble-owned satellite settlements; need fresh food + rotation w/ spires or residents drift toward orc.
- Islands 3%: deep-water fish, isolated, own their land; less orc raiding; lose fish → shift to orc in a few gens.
- Highlands 1%: near Zone, harsh weather, sun & fresh food = healthiest/admired, but more primitive; raiding societies.
- Depths/mines 1%: serfs, debtors, prisoners; some miner-owned claims.
- Free Neither wanderers <0.25%: need filters/gear; distrusted.

## 7. SOCIAL STATUS & WEALTH [CANON core; numbers LEGACY]
Status = wealth = backing. Must spend at your level or lose status. Family/guild money matters more than personal coin.
- Permanent wealth: provided by house/guild (housing, food, gear); lifestyle = social level ±1 by standing. GM may track –99..+99 standing.

Conversely, **Nobles and Wall Merchants are the law's golden children**: while commoners are arrested for brandishing any weapon, nobles get away with drawing and using dueling swords or rapiers with near-total impunity—the law simply assumes it was lawful defense of rank or an affair of honor. **The Strict "Fair Steel" Mandate:** However, a dueling blade **must be standard, mundane steel/iron**. Other weapons owned legally by any free citizen (not enslaved/jailed), but in public must be packed/peace-bonded so not easily drawn. **Concealed Weapons & The Syndicate Annihilation Precedent (Why Concealment is Unthinkable):** Concealing illicit weapons within the Spire is not treated as a minor misdemeanor—it is viewed by Crown authorities as an active act of insurrection. Under established legal enforcement, **one of the primary methods used by the King's Guard and Constabulary to completely purge and eradicate an entire criminal syndicate or Warrens faction is catching its members in possession of concealed weapons.** Because of this existential threat, even the most ruthless underworld bosses and gang leaders—such as **Johnny "Brass-Nails" McGhie** and **"Vanguard" Jack Rourke** in the lower Warrens, or the high-roller operators in Ward 13—strictly enforce absolute weapons compliance among their own operatives. Under the **70/30 Separation Rule**, each Influence class dedicates ~70% of its abilities, syndicates, and contact networks to its primary sphere, while retaining ~30% cross-sphere versatility to branch into the other two social tiers. While most classes can change social class freely without mechanical penalty, **Nobles and Merchants must retain active Status in their respective tier (Status 5+ for Nobles, Status 3+ for Merchants) or lose 3/4 (75%) of all primary domain social connections/favors** (the player chooses which 1/4 remains loyal). **Cartridge & Radiant Converter Module Checkout vs. Gear Carriage:** Only charged Neither cartridges/ammunition and **radiant weapon converter modules** are strictly locked in guardhouses and checked in/out; unloaded weapon chassis and armor are not locked away off-duty. In Spires, radiant weapons are legally mandated to require **converter modules** to couple with and draw power from the municipal radiant grid; these converter modules are regulated and controlled identically to cartridges (illegal for civilians to carry in interior wards without warrants; checked out at gate depots or under high emergency declarations). King's Guard may wear armor and carry weapons/firearms at all times (on/off duty); constables may wear armor and carry service gear while on duty and traveling to/from work; military soldiers may wear/carry weapons and armor when under orders or when using their own personal equipment. Constables rarely have cartridges or converter modules issued on beats (checkout requires high emergency). King's Guard wear a wide spectrum of armor (issued and privately bought by wealthy guards), but all King's Guard armor and weapons are constructed in an unmistakable, distinctive manner clearly marking them as King's Guard gear; unauthorized non-members possessing genuine or counterfeit King's Guard gear are in severe violation of Crown Law (treason/impersonation). "Once a King's Guard, always a King's Guard": unless dishonorably discharged, veterans can be recalled at any time and may retain personally owned King's Guard weapons and armor solely for ceremonial or reactivation use. Armed bodyguards require **King's Rite** (firearms/cartridges/converters; Great Houses, high nobles, Wall Merchants, ministers only) or **Ward-Level Rite** (swords/crossbows; Nobles/Wall Merchants have by default). **Converter Security, Ambient Bleed & Dynamic Frequency Control:** Using any radiant power receiver for a non-intended purpose is illegal, and even more so for weapons: any item that converts radiant energy into a weaponized item outside of legal usage is highly illegal and carries major criminal penalties. Due to ambient radiant bleed from spire utilities, simple bootleg/illicit converters can physically be fabricated to tap low power (civil devices, low-power weapon discharge), though possessing one is a major crime. However, high-energy radiant converters are heavily regulated, closely monitored, and **intentionally manufactured to be extremely large and unwieldy** to prevent concealment. Spires and major generators can periodically alter the pattern of energy release at will, instantly invalidating unauthorized units and requiring synchronized mechanical/resonance adjustments on all high-radiant converters across the grid. **Gunpowder Prohibition:** Forbidden to possess for most citizens; owning a gunpowder gun is legal, but ammunition must be bought and kept at the destination location. Only top noble families with estates above the Zone in high Aither may buy a **King's Rite** allowing guards/members to hold ammunition *within private estates* (Wall Merchants living in Ward 14 below the Zone are strictly excluded due to explosion risk); leaving the property with powder without King's permission = serious crime. **Armor & Departure Rules:** Padded/leather only for everyday civilian spire wear. Concoctions and Clockwork Tech can ONLY heal regular Damage (not Mortal/Critical wounds). Healing must progress from deeper levels first (Step 1 Mortal, then Step 2 Critical, then Damage), with one **PC Exception**: PCs can always heal up to their level in Damage regardless of existing wounds.
