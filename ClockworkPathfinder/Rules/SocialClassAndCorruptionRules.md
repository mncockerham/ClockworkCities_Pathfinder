## 1. Social Class & Status Ladder

In Clockwork Cities, social class is not merely flavor—it dictates a character's legal protections, baseline gear, access to restricted wards, and NPC reactions.

### What Determines Class & Where You May Live

Social class and residence are related, but they are not the same. A person's wealth, clothing, or address does not by itself grant a new institutional or hereditary class.

- **Merchant, Noble & Royal Distinctions:** In ordinary practice, Noble and Royal class distinctions are hereditary, acquired by birth or recognized marriage into the class. Merchant status has a resource-based entry point: a person with sufficient resources and standing—shown by ownership of one or more significant commercial assets, such as an inn, warehouse, or skyship—can reach the base Merchant level without being born into a Merchant House. Wall Merchant status is usually dynastic, but on rare occasions a new house may replace a failing Wall Merchant house. A noble whose wealth and standing fall far enough may descend into Merchant class; their former noble title ends and no longer exists. Such families commonly relocate to another Spire to escape the social shame. **The King's word is law:** a royal decree or act can establish any status or legal truth, regardless of ordinary rules. Only rebellion can stop it; social expectations may constrain the King's choices, but not his authority.
- **Wall Merchant Scale:** Wall Merchants are robber barons whose holdings can include fleets of airships, multiple warhorses, territory in other Spires, and royal grants of mines or hamlets. These are examples of scale, not a required checklist.
- **Military & University Status:** Military class comes from current service; University class comes from being a student or instructor. These statuses depend on the qualifying role, not simply on wealth or family background.
- **Ward 13 and Lower Residential Mobility:** Citizens may move freely among Ward 13 and lower wards if they can secure a lease and afford the ongoing lifestyle costs appropriate to that residence. Their circumstances may move them upward or downward as those costs become affordable or not. A higher-cost address or lifestyle does not itself confer Merchant, Noble, Royal, Military, or University status.
- **Residence Does Not Override Access Rules:** Freedom to choose a home within Ward 13 and lower does not waive normal transit fares, security checks, or role-based restrictions on institutional spaces and dedicated routes.

### Parental Conferred Status & Lineage
- **Baseline Lineage:** For the vast majority of citizens (laborers, freemen, craftsmen, soldiers, junior merchants, academics), children start at the **exact same rank as their parents**.
- **The Leadership Caveat:** When parents hold a **leadership role** in an organization, dynasty, or guild (e.g. Grand Guildmaster, House Patriarch, Fortress Commandant, Merchant Baron), the PC **starts just below them** (reflecting junior family/cadet standing rather than unearned apex executive control).
- **Marriage & Adoption:** Characters who buy higher status through Edge Points are canonically explained via marriage, formal adoption, or parental standing; unaffiliated characters need only a simple one-sentence explanation.

### Ward Travel, Escort & Formal Summons
- **The Ward Sponsor Rule:** Anyone traveling into a ward **alongside a companion whose base status equals or exceeds that ward's entry threshold** may travel freely through that ward, provided all required **wealth fees, gate tolls, and ward transit tariffs are fully covered by the group**.
- **Formal Summons:** A formal, verified **summons** issued by a recognized ward magistrate, civic department, guildmaster, or noble house permits the summoned party to pass through restricted ward checkpoints directly to their destination.

### Falls from Grace: The Two Mechanisms (Swift, Brutal & Public)
A fall from social standing is swift, brutal, and completely public—there is no private cushioning or grace period. A fall is always triggered by **one of two things**:
1. **Social Censure from Crime or Cultural Violations:** The character commits an illegal act, betrays institutional trust, or violates severe high-caste cultural taboos. Their former class immediately disavows them to safeguard their own reputations, revoking audience, warrants, and civic standing.
2. **Failure to Pay Bills & Complete Abandonment:** The character cannot pay their debts, ward rents, or guild fees, and their **entire social circle refuses to float or support them any longer**. (If you had friends in that class who truly cared or had the leverage to bail you out, you wouldn't be having this problem). The character is forced to **liquidate their possessions, sell fine gear, and physically move down to a lower ward and level of living**.

### Status, Access & Understanding: Talking vs. Information
- **Access Granted by Status:** If sufficient status exists, **doors open automatically**. A high-status character can enter elite salons, private clubs, and council chambers, and high-ranking peers will grant audience and pleasantries without gatekeeping.
- **Talking vs. Extracting Information:** Having access and conversing is **vastly different from extracting genuine information**. High-ranking peers guard sensitive secrets, trade numbers, and court conspiracies closely; extracting actionable intel still requires leveraging **Understanding, real-person contacts, reciprocal favors, or social maneuvers**.

### Sword Rights: Firm Law & Custom (No Workarounds)
- **Zero Loopholes:** Law and custom regarding Sword Rights are completely firm. **No workarounds exist.**
  - **Lesser Nobility (Status 55–60):** Do **NOT** possess sword rights under any legal theory, post, or pretext.
  - **Standard Nobility (Status 65–75), Wall Merchants (80–90), and Great Houses (85–95):** Possess full legal sword rights and authorization to open-carry dueling blades in public wards.
  - Any attempt by lesser nobility or unentitled merchants to carry dueling swords via honorary commissions or legal dodges results in immediate weapon confiscation and punitive fines by the King's Guard.

### Status Mechanics & Upkeep
- **Lifestyle Upkeep:** Characters must expend wealth commensurate with their status tier each month. Failing to maintain appearances causes a loss of status tier or standing within their house/guild.
- **Institutional Backing & Downward Reclassification:** If an artisan leaves their guild or a soldier deserts, their status immediately drops to Citizen (Status 1) or Indentured (Status –1), as appropriate. If a Merchant House fails, its members lose the backing and standing tied to it; individuals with enough independent resources may retain or qualify for base Merchant status, while others fall to Citizen or lower. Nobles whose wealth and standing fall below what their rank can sustain may be reclassified as Merchants; their noble title ceases to exist, and many relocate to another Spire to avoid the shame of their fall.
- **Class Fluidity vs. Mandatory Status Restrictions (Noble & Merchant Only):**
  - **The Universal Rule:** Across the entire game, **the only classes with mandatory social class restrictions are the Noble and Merchant**. All other classes (Soldier, Scout, Bombardier, Clockwork Engineer, Chirurgeon, Pugilist, Rake, Scholar, Survivor, Officer, Clockwork Rifleman, Grey Marketeer) can be played across any social stratum (Status $\le 0$ to 100) without losing class features or combat efficacy.
  - **Noble & Merchant Ability Loss & Forfeiture of Sword Rights:**
    - **Nobles (Mandatory Status 55+):** Must maintain Noble status. If stripped of rank, disowned, bankrupted, or demoted below Status 55, they retain combat feats and skills, but suffer:
      1. **Immediate Loss of 3/4 (75%) Connections:** 75% of high-society patrons, court contacts, and council favors refuse audience. The **player chooses which 1/4 (25%) remains loyal**.
      2. **Revocation of Legal Sword Rights:** If falling below Status 65 (Standard Nobility threshold), their **legal right to openly carry a dueling sword (rapier/smallsword) in public wards is revoked immediately**. Constables will confiscate the weapon or arrest them.
      3. **Suspension of Sovereign Features:** Writs, high-status directives, and noble retinues become dormant until status is reclaimed.
    - **Merchants (Mandatory Status 30+ / Wall Merchant 80+):** Must maintain solvency and guild standing. If defaulted, blacklisted, or bankrupted below Status 30 (or below 80 for Wall Merchants), they retain basic feats, but suffer:
      1. **Immediate Loss of 3/4 (75%) Commercial Connections:** 75% of trade credits, warehouse lines, and guild favors freeze. The **player chooses which 1/4 (25%) remains loyal**.
      2. **Revocation of Sword Rights & Retainers (Wall Merchants):** If falling below Status 80, **Wall Merchants immediately lose their legal sword rights and the legal right to field armed private retainers** in public wards.
      3. **Suspension of Credit & Syndicate Abilities:** Lines of Credit, wholesale networks, and commercial retinues freeze until solvency or guild licensure is restored.
- **Social Friction:** Characters interacting with social strata significantly above or below their own suffer circumstance penalties to social skill checks (Diplomacy, Deception, Intimidation) proportional to the gap, unless introduced by a recognized patron.

### Institutional Legacy & Lineage Succession (Mitigating the Cost of Character Death)
In a setting where 15% of encounters are lethal Boss Crucibles and 70% are grinding attrition fights, character death is an inevitable, looming reality. To ensure players do not lose the institutional and political progress they have spent months advancing, Clockwork Cities features the **Lineage Succession System**:
- **The Core Premise:** *"Individuals die; the House, the Guild, and the Regiment endure."*
- **Encouraged (But Not Mandatory) House/Guild Continuity:**
  - When a character dies, the player is **strongly encouraged (though never forced)** to create their next character within the same **Family House, Trade Guild, Military Regiment, or Syndicate**.
  - *Example:* If an Artisan smith dies in the deep Zone, their replacement might be their ambitious journeyman apprentice, an older brother serving as an engineer in the same guild, or a cousin from a branch workshop sent to settle the estate.
- **What Is Preserved (The Inherited Legacy):**
  - **Faction Standing & Favors:** The new character inherits the deceased character's accumulated **Standing (-99 to +99)** and institutional trust within that sponsor, modified by a minor "re-proving" tax (starting at the old Standing –10, minimum 0).
  - **Permanent Institutional Wealth:** Housing rights, laboratory access, workshop benches, carriage allotments, and guild credit lines remain fully intact and immediately transfer to the successor.
  - **The Transitory Hoard Rule:** The deceased character's **House-issued gear** (chassis, military rifles, armor) is officially reclaimed by the sponsor and re-issued to the successor without cost. Only personal transitory pocket cash and unbonded street gear are lost or left with the corpse.
  - **Contact & Understanding Hand-off:** The successor automatically inherits up to **half (rounded down)** of the deceased character's specific social contacts and Focus Understandings, representing family diaries, shared workshop ledgers, or briefing notes passed through the institution.
- **Freedom to Pivot:** If the player chooses *not* to continue the lineage and instead rolls an entirely new character from a different social tier (e.g., abandoning the Machinists' Guild to play a Warrens Survivor or an outland Scout), they start fresh at standard party level and baseline wealth for that new social tier, while their previous house remains in the world as potential allies or rivals.

### Quick Reference: Total Shift Score (Air + Food)
- **Air Shift Levels:** Integer steps (0 to 5) determined by biological distance from your Heritage's native layer.
- **Food Shift Levels:** 0.5-point increments based on diet (e.g. 50% Mela + 50% raw Sycelium = 0.5 Shift).
- **Combined Penalty Track:**
  - **Score 0.0:** Equilibrium (normal healing/rest).
  - **Score 0.5–1.0:** Mild Imbalance (rest healing takes 50% longer, –1 Fortitude vs. disease).
  - **Score 1.5–2.0:** Chronic Degradation (*Clumsy 1* or *Enfeebled 1*, resting HP recovery halved).
  - **Score 2.5–3.0:** Severe Cellular Stress (*Clumsy 1*, *Enfeebled 1*, *Stupefied 1*).
  - **Score 3.5–4.0:** Acute Breakdown (*Drained 1*, *Stupefied 2*, weekly Fortitude save vs. *Acute Neither Sickness*).
  - **Score 4.5+:** Systemic Failure / Mutation (*Drained 2*, *Doomed 1*; 3 months = Aberrant Speciation into Goblins, Neanderthals, or Troglodytes).

### Environmental Mitigations
- **Architecture:** Standard Neithercrete rooms reduce effective Air Shift by **1 Level**; hermetic deep vaults reduce by **2 Levels**.
- **Sealed Respirators:** Oiled leather filters reduce effective Air Shift by **1 Level** for up to 8 hours.
- **Storm Depth:** Mine depth reduces Neither Storm intensity by **1 Level per 100 feet** (at 300+ feet, storm damage is completely negated).
