# Non-Player Characters (NPCs) — Clockwork Cities Rules

## The Nature of Combat & Encounters
Unlike traditional fantasy roleplaying games and standard Pathfinder where adventuring parties frequently battle mythical beasts, dragons, fiends, and undead, the vast majority of conflicts in Clockwork Cities take place between **people**. Encounters are fundamentally grounded in human struggles: clashes with ward constables, street gangs, rival syndicates, labor strikers, Spire enforcers, political assassins, and corporate mercenaries.

Even when non-human "monsters" are encountered, they are not fantastical creatures born of supernatural planar dimensions or folklore. Instead, they are **real-world fauna and flora that have suffered Neither Corruption**:
- **Neither-Corrupted Fauna:** Familiar animals—such as wolves, dogs, bats, vermin, boars, and predatory birds—that have endured prolonged exposure to mutagenic Neither fog, resulting in abnormal size, aggressive behavioral frenzy, bio-luminescent veins, hardened bony carapaces, caustic saliva, or altered sensory organs.
- **Neither-Corrupted Flora:** Invasive weeds, briars, fungal colonies, and creeping vines adapted to feed on Neither vapor and industrial runoff, developing toxic spore clouds, rapid constriction reflexes, or corrosive sap.
- **Automata & Engineered Threats:** Beyond corrupted biological life, non-human opponents are almost exclusively man-made: clockwork sentries, automated perimeter turrets, steam-powered work engines turned lethal, or unstable Neither-tech prototypes.

## NPC Classes & Levels
NPCs in Clockwork Cities can exist as **any class and any level**. A 12th-level Scholar, an 8th-level Artisan, a 4th-level Soldier, or a 1st-level Survivor are all fully valid characters within the world. Class and level represent their training, expertise, cognitive acumen, or professional dedication.

However, high level or class competence does not inherently mean someone has been hardened by real, life-or-death violence or harsh wilderness survival. To reflect the insulating safety of sheltered urban life versus the grueling brutality of the outside world, NPCs can possess two unique traits/tags: **Civilized** and **Protected**.

### The Civilized & Protected Tags

These tags represent individuals who have led relatively sheltered lives, isolated from extreme physical hardship, mortal peril, or lethal violence. 

> [!NOTE]
> **Storyteller Baseline & Narrative Flexibility:**
> The limitations detailed below represent the **common restrictions** on Civilized and Protected NPCs. They exist to allow high-level, masterfully skilled individuals (such as master surgeons, chief accountants, or political theorists) to inhabit the world with credible high-tier skill bonuses without inadvertently turning them into high-HP, combat-deadly juggernauts. 
> 
> However, **these are guidelines, not rigid dogmas**. If it serves the story, Storytellers are explicitly encouraged to customize or limit how many of these restrictions apply to any given NPC depending on their personal narrative arc, secret background, or specific role in an encounter.

- **Distribution & Environment:**
  - **Inside Spires & Cities:** The vast majority of citizens fall into one of these two categories, depending on their background and line of work. For example, a laborer who has spent their entire adult life cultivating indoor fungus farms in a controlled, enclosed city ward almost always possesses one or both of these tags.
  - **Profession Discrepancies:** Soldiers, constabulary enforcers, and street toughs seldom carry these tags even within a Spire. Conversely, Scholars, Artisans, Dandies, and clerical bureaucrats frequently carry them.
  - **Outside Spires & Cities:** These tags are exceptionally rare outside of cities or among professions whose work regularly exposes them to the Neither fog, rail outriding, subterranean excavation, or wilderness borders.

#### Tag Definitions & Mechanics

- **[Civilized]**
  - *Context:* The individual has lived within the social order, civic stability, and daily routine of civilization. They are unaccustomed to physical violence, genuine life-or-death shock, or raw survival hardship.
  - *Stat Block & Character Sheet Limits:*
    - **Reduced Attribute Boosts:** They receive only **half the normal attribute boosts** at character creation and level advancement (typically 2 boosts instead of 4 at milestone levels, favoring mental/technical attributes).
    - **Hit Point Progression Cap:** They do **not** gain Constitution modifier bonuses to their maximum Hit Points when leveling up. Regardless of their class base HP, they gain only a flat **+2 Hit Points per level** beyond 1st level.
    - **Proficiency Ceiling:** Their combat training rarely exceeds civilian self-defense; they cannot advance beyond **Trained** in weapons or armor, regardless of class level.
    - **No Combat Reactions:** They lack seasoned battlefield reflexes and **cannot use combat reactions** (such as *Reactive Strike* or defensive parries) triggered by hostile movements or attacks.
    - **Physical Feat Restrictions:** They should **seldom have feats** that increase physical statistics, raw toughness, athletic endurance, or martial reflexes (such as *Toughness*, *Diehard*, or reactive counters), reflecting an unconditioned body.
  - *Situational Mechanics:*
    - **Trauma Collapse (Mortal Wounds):** The moment a Civilized NPC suffers their first point of **Mortal damage** (crossing below the Step 2 threshold per [`AISummary/20_Healing.md`](file:///c:/Users/mncoc/OneDrive/Documents/Git/ClockworkCities_Pathfinder/AISummary/20_Healing.md)), they collapse into near-complete uselessness. They drop held items and curl up in terror and agony. They take no voluntary actions on their turn unless an ally within 30 feet spends **1 Action to Command or Rally** them (auditory); even when directed, they act at a –2 circumstance penalty and cannot take hostile actions.
    - **Combat Hesitation:** In the first round of an unexpected or lethal combat, a Civilized creature that has not acted yet takes a –2 circumstance penalty to Initiative and saving throws against Fear or Demoralize effects.
    - **Harsh Environment Vulnerability:** When exposed to wilderness hazards, environmental Neither exposure, or severe deprivation (starvation, thirst, extreme temperatures without Spire heating/cooling), their saving throws are one degree of success worse than rolled.
    - **Civil Composure:** While within their familiar urban environment and operating under civil law, they gain a +1 circumstance bonus to Lore and Society checks regarding civic procedures, local commerce, and routine trade.

- **[Protected]**
  - *Context:* The individual has lived an existence separated even further from the harshness and realities of life. For whatever reason, their entire existence has been sheltered and curated—for instance, high-born nobles who buy their way into military commissions, have never faced a difficult or perilous assignment, and were sparred with handpicked armsmen instructed never to let them lose.
  - *Stat Block & Character Sheet Limits:*
    - **Extreme Attribute Restrictions:** When receiving attribute adjustments, they receive **only 1 attribute boost** at character milestones, regardless of what class advancement normally dictates.
    - **Hit Point Progression Floor:** Constitution modifier scores are **never applied** to their Hit Points. Regardless of class, they receive only a flat **+1 Hit Point per level** beyond 1st level.
    - **Untrained in True Combat:** They are **Untrained** in martial weapons and medium/heavy armor; at best they are Trained with light parade weapons (dueling canes, ceremonial rapiers, or holdout derringers). Heavy armors or high-recoil weapons impose an immediate Clumsy 2 condition.
    - **No Combat Reactions:** They **cannot take combat reactions** of any kind.
    - **Feat Restrictions:** They are virtually barred from selecting physical conditioning, survival, or combat grit feats, reflecting artificial training and utter lack of real-world trial.
  - *Situational Mechanics:*
    - **Premature Trauma Collapse (Critical Wounds):** Because of their extreme sheltered fragility, a Protected NPC collapses early: the moment they suffer their first point of **Critical damage** (crossing below the regular Damage threshold per [`AISummary/20_Healing.md`](file:///c:/Users/mncoc/OneDrive/Documents/Git/ClockworkCities_Pathfinder/AISummary/20_Healing.md)), they collapse into uselessness, curling into a ball or freezing in blind panic. They take no independent actions and require an ally to spend **1 Action to Command/Rally** them to perform even simple retreat or seek cover.
    - **Morale Ripple (Loss of Figurehead):** If a Protected NPC collapses or panics in front of subordinates (bodyguards, retainers, junior employees, or lower-standing troops), all allied subordinates must immediately succeed at a **Will saving throw against Demoralize** (DC equal to the triggering attack's DC or standard level DC) or become **Frightened 1** as their leadership and sponsor buckles.
    - **Shock of Violence:** The first time a Protected character takes damage in an encounter (even regular Damage from firearms, concussive blasts, or bladed weapons), they become **Frightened 1** (or increase their Frightened value by 1) as physical panic and disbelief set in.
    - **Fragile Fortitude:** Their wound thresholds and recovery checks against mortal bleeding or infectious exposure suffer a –2 penalty unless treated immediately in a sterile, indoor environment.
    - **Entitled Authority:** Against creatures of equal or lower Social Standing who operate within the same legal or corporate hierarchy, a Protected NPC gains a +1 circumstance bonus to Coerce or Command, relying on institutional power and retinues to enforce compliance.

### Storyteller / GM Discretion & Individual Dedication
The restrictions outlined for both **Civilized** and **Protected** NPCs provide a baseline for portraying sheltered populations realistically in a high-lethality, steampunk world. However, **Storytellers may freely adjust or relax these restrictions**—allowing more attribute boosts or higher Hit Points—whenever an NPC's specific narrative arc, secret training, or dramatic importance to the story warrants it.

> **Individual Dedication & Mindset:**
> These tags vary heavily by person, personal grit, and dedication rather than pedigree alone. For instance, consider twin siblings born to nobility who received identical arms training:
> - If the first twin threw tantrums whenever they lost and their instructors were pressured to coddle and protect them, they inevitably develop with the **[Protected]** tag.
> - If the other twin—despite being equally spoiled and wealthy—insisted on rigorous, honest sparring and pushed through bruising defeats, they might use the **standard rules** for leveling, attribute boosts, and Hit Points.

### Shedding Tags & Seasoning Through Adversity
The **[Civilized]** and **[Protected]** tags are **not permanent biological conditions**. When sheltered people are thrust into genuine danger, forced to endure prolonged hardship, or actively choose to face trial without their safety nets, they can gradually outgrow these limitations:

- **Stepping Down:** A character with the **[Protected]** tag who survives real combat, outriding, or harsh field conditions often sheds the Protected tag first, transitioning into **[Civilized]** as their initial shock and fragility wear off.
- **Full Hardening (Standard Progression):** Continued exposure to survival challenges, rigorous combat experience, and life-or-death stakes eventually removes the **[Civilized]** tag entirely. From that point forward, the character levels using normal class rules:
  - They gain standard attribute boost progressions on future milestones.
  - They gain standard class Hit Points plus Constitution bonuses on subsequent level-ups (and at GM discretion, may retroactively or incrementally recover lost Hit Point maximums through intense physical conditioning downtime).
  - They gain access to standard physical and combat grit feats.

## NPC Framework & Design Principles
- **Mundane & Technical Competencies:** Rather than spellcaster levels or supernatural spell-like abilities, specialized NPCs wield Neither-tech weaponry, chemical grenades, surgical injectables, or command auras.
- **Social Class & Standing:** An NPC's social standing heavily determines their access to military-grade cartridges, civil immunity, legal recourse, and authority across wards.
- **Creature & Humanoid Roles:**
  - *Civilian / Working Class:* Artisans, Scholars, Miners, and factory hands with specialized skill mastery and tool proficiency.
  - *Enforcers & Guards:* Constables, Officers, and Ward Militia utilizing gunpowder firearms, shields, and crowd-control tactics.
  - *Underworld & Fringe:* Scavengers, Survivors, and Mad Bombers operating in lower wards and Neither-side borders.
  - *High Society & Oligarchs:* Dandies and Merchants leveraging hirelings, legal retainers, and deep purse reserves.
