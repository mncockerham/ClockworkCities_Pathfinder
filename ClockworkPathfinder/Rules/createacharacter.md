## 1. Overview & Best Creation Order

Creating a character in Clockwork Cities follows the proven Pathfinder 2e Remaster structure, adjusted for the secular steampunk setting, the 12-Point Edge system, and institutional backing.

Follow these 7 steps in order for the smoothest character creation experience:

```
[1. Character Concept] ──> [2. Allocate 12 Edge Points] ──> [3. Choose Biological Heritage]
                                                                        │
[6. Starting Gear & Bulwark] <── [5. Attributes & Skills] <── [4. Choose Character Class]
          │
[7. Final Details & Background Story]
```

> [!WARNING]
> **The Mandatory Class Status Restrictions: Noble & Merchant Only**
> Across the entire game, **only the Noble and Merchant character classes have mandatory social class prerequisites and loss-of-ability penalties**. All other classes (Soldier, Scout, Clockwork Engineer, Scholar, Chirurgeon, Clockwork Rifleman, Bombardier, Pugilist, Rake, Survivor, Officer) can be played freely across any social stratum from Status $\le 0$ to 95 without losing class abilities or features.
> - **Noble (Mandatory Floor Status 55+):** Must start at Status 55–60 (Lesser Noble) or higher. If a Noble's status falls below this threshold during play, they retain general combat feats, but **immediately lose 3/4 (75%) of their high-society contacts and council favors** (the player chooses the 25% that remain loyal). If their status falls below Status 65, **they immediately forfeit their legal sword rights** (openly carrying a dueling blade in public becomes an arrestable crime).
> - **Merchant (Mandatory Floor Status 30+ / Wall Merchant Floor 80+):** Must start at Status 30–70 (Standard Merchant) or Status 80–90 (Wall Merchant). If a Merchant falls below Status 30 (bankruptcy/blacklisting), they retain combat feats, but **immediately lose 3/4 (75%) of their trade credit, supplier networks, and commercial connections** (the player chooses the 25% that remain loyal). If a Wall Merchant falls below Status 80, **they immediately forfeit both their legal sword rights and the right to field armed corporate retainers** in Spire wards.
> - **Grey Marketeer (Enterprise Scope, Not a Status Restriction):** The Grey Marketeer does not have a hard status minimum (they can start at Status $\le 0$), but their clientele is bounded: your personal status cannot be **more than 3 class tiers lower** than the target social bracket of your primary clientele:
>   - *Selling to Standard Merchants (Status 30–70):* Minimum **Status $\le 0$ (Non-Citizen / Warrens Outcast)**.
>   - *Selling to Lesser Nobles (Status 55–60):* Minimum **Status 6–10 (Physical Labor)**.
>   - *Selling to Standard Nobles (Status 65–75):* Minimum **Status 21–25 (Apprentices / Recruits)**.
>   - *Selling to Wall Merchants (Status 80–90) & Great Houses (Status 85–95):* Minimum **Status 30+ (Established Merchant / Master Broker)**.

## Step 1: Formulate Your Character Concept
Before opening menus, determine who your character is and where they fit in the Spire:
- Are you an indentured foundry hand or a subterranean tunnel digger?
- A skilled guild clockmaker or university medical student?
- A disgraced noble officer seeking redemption, or an alley-smart Warrens survivor?

---

## Step 2: Allocate Your 12 Edge Points (Social Rank & Personal Edges)
Every character starts with **12 Edge Points** (`[edges.md]`). Spend them now:

1. **Choose Your Starting Social Class Rank:**
   - Through **parental lineage** (starting at the same rank as your parents, or just below them if the parent is an organizational/dynasty leader), **marriage, or formal adoption**, characters can hold any social status they purchase. If you are not born, adopted, or married into a recognized house, firm, or guild, you need only provide a simple 1-sentence in-world explanation for how you hold that rank.
   - **Dormant Higher Status & Player Control of Restoration:** A character **can buy a higher social status than the station they currently inhabit** (e.g. purchasing Noble or Wall Merchant status while currently living in the Warrens as a disowned heir). Reclaiming lost or dormant status that was paid for is **vastly easier than climbing unearned ranks**, and **the player retains total control over how and when their character reclaims their proper status** during the campaign.
   - **Non-Citizen / Warrens Outcast ($\le 0$):** 0 Points (12 pts remaining)
   - **Physical Labor (Unskilled / Zero Trust, Status 6–10):** 1 Point (11 pts remaining)
   - **Trusted Laborer (Status 11–15):** 2 Points (10 pts remaining)
   - **Freeman / Freewoman (Unaffiliated, Status 16–20):** 5 Points (7 pts remaining — *high income, no guild/feudal obligations*)
   - **Apprentice / Military Recruit (Status 21–25):** 3 Points (9 pts remaining)
   - **Guild Craftsman / Artisan (Status 26–55):** 3 Points (9 pts remaining)
   - **Military Regular / Beat Constable (Status 26–65):** 3 Points (9 pts remaining)
   - **Standard Merchant (Status 30–70):** 3 Points (9 pts remaining)
   - **Academia / University Collegium (Status 45–70):** 5 Points (7 pts remaining)
   - **Lesser Nobility (Working Gentry, Status 55–60):** 6 Points (6 pts remaining — *no sword rights*)
   - **Standard Nobility (Landed Peers, Status 65–75):** 8 Points (4 pts remaining — *includes sword rights*)
   - **Wall Merchant (Status 80–90):** 9 Points (3 pts remaining — *includes sword rights & retainers*)
   - **Great House Noble (Status 85–95):** 10 Points (2 pts remaining — *includes sword rights & retainers*)

2. **Spend Remaining Points on Personal Edges (Single-Purchase Only):**
   - **Tactical Edges:** Armor Step-Up (3 pts, max Expert), AP Battery Surge (4 pts: +1 AP at Lv 1, 5, 15), Hardened Frame (+HP / Diehard, 2 pts).
   - **Heritage / Biological Edges:** Iron-Gut Poison Immunity (2 pts), Neither-Conditioned Lungs (3 rerolls vs Neither, 2 pts), Attribute Boost (+1 to STR/DEX/CON, 4 pts), Workhorse Bulk (1 pt), Half-Elf Static/Rigging traits (1–2 pts), Dwarf Stone Anchor/Sapper traits (1–3 pts).
   - **Practical Edges:** Extra Trained Skill (1 pt), Bonus 1st-Level Feat (2 pts), Jury-Rigger (2 pts), Street Anonymity (1 pt).

## Step 3: Choose Your Biological Heritage (Humanity Spectrum)
Select one of the six human environmental adaptations (`[AncestriesAndHeritages.md]`):
- **Human (Upper Neither):** The versatile center; adaptable everywhere, broad feat options.
- **Orc (Lower Neither):** Dense bone, muscle bulk, stonecrafting, delayed storm reactions.
- **Half-Elf (The Zone):** Slender, barometrically adapted to altitude, airships, and static lightning.
- **Dwarf (Lightless Neither):** Subterranean sappers, dense stature, reflective pupil flash recovery.
- **High Elf (The Aither) / Deep One (The Deep):** Specialized extreme-strata heritages (require life support or closed sanctuaries outside native environments).

*Heritage Details:* Record your base Hit Points (typically 8 HP for Human/Half-Elf, 10 HP for Orc/Dwarf), Size (Medium), Speed (25–30 ft), and initial Ancestry Feat.

---

## Step 4: Choose Your Character Class
Select your class from the 13 core playable classes (ClockworkPathfinder/Classes/):
- **Frontline Combatants:** Soldier, Pugilist, Scout.
- **Marksmen & Heavy Ballistics:** Clockwork Rifleman (incorporates heavy ordnance and bombardier tracks).
- **Technical & Scientific:** Clockwork Engineer (incorporates master crafting & gear conditioning), Chirurgeon, Scholar (incorporates forensic investigator & inquisitor doctrines).
- **Command & Leaders:** Officer.
- **Influence & Social Intrigue:** Dandy / Rake, Grey Marketeer, Survivor, plus **Noble** and **Merchant** *(see rank warning above)*.
*(Note: Civilian archetypes like Commoner, Craftsman, Laborer, Professional, and Sailor (Air & Sea) are weaker NPC support classes tuned for world-building, while wild monstrosities use Creature Classes).*
*Class Details:* Record your Key Attribute, class Hit Points per level, initial proficiencies (Perception, Saves, Attacks, Defenses), 1st-level Class Feat, and class features.

---

## Step 5: Assign Attributes & Starting Skills
Clockwork Cities uses standard Pathfinder 2e Remaster attribute boosts (`[Player Core p. 20]`):

1. **Calculate Attributes (STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, CHA):**
   - Start with an initial baseline of +0 across all attributes.
   - Apply Ancestry boosts (typically two free boosts or heritage boosts).
   - Apply Background boosts (one from your trade/origin, one free).
   - Apply Class Key Attribute boost (+1 to your class key stat).
   - Apply 4 Free Attribute Boosts (each applied to a different attribute).
   - *(Optional: Apply the +1 Edge Attribute Boost if purchased in Step 2).*
   - *Cap:* No attribute can exceed +4 at 1st level.
2. **Assign Starting Skills & Social Skills (`Understanding`):**
   - Select your trained standard PF2e skills (Athletics, Stealth, Crafting, Medicine, etc.) based on your class baseline.
   - Choose your starting **Social Skills (`Understanding`)** per `[Skills.md]`: 
     - Choose your Broad Institutional Groups (Trade Guilds, Military, Underworld, Nobility, etc.).
     - Choose each entry's specificity tier: *Broad (+0), Sub-Group (+1), Specific Place (+2), or Specific Person (+3)*.

---

## Step 6: Select Starting Gear & Citizen Bulwark Slots
Unlike standard fantasy games with bags of random gold, gear is governed by your institutional backing (`[ResourcesAndWealth.md]`):

1. **Starting Personal Funds:**
   - Record your monthly spending allowance based on your Social Status tier (e.g., Status 1 = 2 Nobels, Status 3 = 20 Nobels, Status 5 = 100 Nobels).
2. **Select Your 3 Citizen Bulwark Slots (Player Choice):**
   - Every citizen has the recognized right to retain and maintain **any 3 core items of your choice** (weapons, armor, or NeitherTeck devices) that can never be permanently confiscated or stolen under ordinary Spire law.
   - Equip your basic class weapons, ammunition cartridges, and armor.
   - *(Note: Ensure your weapons comply with Spire carrying laws—pack weapons in cases when traveling through public wards, or carry a mundane steel dueling rapier if you hold noble/merchant status).*

## Step 7: Final Calculations & Background Story
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Standard PF2e Bonus Stacking Enforcement [Player Core p.12]:**
> When calculating final bonuses from feats, edges, weapons, items, and abilities: **if two effects grant the same bonus type (Circumstance, Item, or Status) to the same statistic, only use the highest value**. They never stack unless an ability explicitly states it is a specific exception. Untyped bonuses to the same check or defense do not stack.

Wrap up your character sheet:
- **Armor Class (AC):** $10 + \text{DEX mod (up to cap)} + \text{Armor Item Bonus} + \text{Proficiency Bonus}$.
- **Hit Points & Combat Box Setup (AISummary/20_Healing.md):**
  - Calculate your 3 Health Tiers: **Regular Damage**, **Critical Wounds**, and **Mortal Wounds** using your Step size ($\lfloor 	ext{Max HP}/4 
floor$, or flat 3/3 for HP $\le 16$).
  - Record the Max and Current values for all three tiers on your sheet.
  - At any time and before combat, conduct a quick 30-second Health Audit to verify that any days rested, morning Chirurgeon concoctions received, or items used earlier were actually marked down. Set up your **Active Combat Box**: $\text{Available HP} = \text{Current Regular} + \text{Current Critical} + \text{Current Mortal}$. Track incoming damage as a single running **Total Damage** tally, noting any nonlethal points in a **Nonlethal Pool**. At negative HP, compare the two to see if you are stable or dying. Post-combat: heal damage equal to current nonlethal, apply remaining damage downward (Current Regular -> Current Critical -> Current Mortal), and record any resulting health tier wound penalties on your character sheet.
- **Active Power (AP):** Record your starting Active Surge pool (Tier 0 Martials = 0 AP; Tier 1 Operatives = 0 AP; Influence = 1 AP; Technical/Engineers = 2–3 AP; add +1 AP if you purchased the *AP Battery Surge Edge*).
- **Character Story (The Simple Personal Anecdote):** Write 1 or 2 simple sentences explaining how your character picked up their skills or unusual traits (no long essays required).

## Step 8: Institutional Lineage Succession (Handling Character Death)
Because Clockwork Cities features deep institutional backing (*"Worth = who you represent"*), permanent character death does not erase a player's hard-earned social capital:
- **The Succession Rule:** When a character suffers permanent death, the player creates their replacement character as an **institutional or familial successor**:
  - A younger sibling, junior cousin, or recognized ward heir (Nobles/Merchants).
  - A trusted apprentice, journeyman, or laboratory assistant (Clockwork Engineers/Scholars/Chirurgeons).
  - A squad corporal, sworn shield-bearer, or battlefield protégé (Soldiers/Officers).
  - A trusted street runner, partner, or syndicate protégé (Survivors/Grey Marketeers).
- **Inherited Assets & Standing:** The replacement character inherits the deceased character's **institutional standing, guild charters, family titles, leased quarters, and recovered personal gear**. 
- **Party Continuity:** This ensures that the party's social clout, legal standing, and ongoing story arcs continue seamlessly without resetting the player to a penniless outcast.

You are now ready to enter the Great Spires and the Neither wastelands!
