## 1. Aberrant Sub-Types & Biological Divergence

While the vast majority of humanity across the world belongs to the primary spectrum of six continuous adaptations (High Elf, Half-Elf, Human, Orc, Dwarf, and Deep One), violent environmental instability, dietary trauma, and extreme atmospheric saturation have splintered off several **aberrant sub-types**. 

Nearly all aberrant lineages are damaged or mutated offshoots of **Type 3 (Upper Neither Human)** or **Type 4 (Lower Neither Orc)**, who together constitute over 90% of all humanoid life.

### Sub-Type Classification Matrix

| Sub-Type | Biological Origin | Primary Catalyst | Core Physiological Condition | Estimated Global Population |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 3a / 4c: Goblin** | Human (Type 3) or Orc (Type 4) | Long-term chronic Neither exposure (Humans) or rare chronic Aither exposure (Orcs); areas of high flux | Severe degradation of physical stature and intellect; breeds true in high-flux transition corridors and Warrens | ~200,000 |
| **Type 3b: Vampire** | Human (Type 3) | Inability to metabolize Aither/clean food; active or dormant reaction triggered by cannibalism | Metabolic decay, tissue atrophy; halted or reversed only by consuming blood of Type 1–3 humans (Orc blood provides partial relief) | ~5,000 |
| **Type 4a: Beast** | Orc (Type 4) | Origin unknown; hyper-aggressive neurological breakdown | Severe degradation of intellect and instinctual control; reversed temporarily by consuming humanoid flesh (Types 1–5) | ~10,000 |
| **Type 4b: Neanderthal** | Orc (Type 4) | Severe deprivation of Neither-corrupted sustenance (starved of native Neither food) | Permanent physical and neural scarring; unable to fully sustain Orc bulk. Reverts to Human or recovers to Orc across generations | ~20,000 |
| **Troglodyte** | Orc (Type 4) / Goblin | Generationally stranded in the sunless, deepest ruined Tombs; Dwarf-layer drift | Complete loss of pigmentation, blind/cat-optic shift, predatory feral adaptation | ~10,000 |

## 2. Rare Heritages (Player Character Rules)

These rare heritages function identically to versatile heritages or specific heritages chosen at 1st level during character creation. They modify the base Pathfinder 2e Human chassis `[Player Core p.44]`.

---

### Rare Heritage: Goblin (Warren-Bred / Flux-Born)
*Humans or Orcs whose bloodlines suffered prolonged environmental flux or suffocating Neither saturation in the deep municipal Warrens, stunting their stature and wiring their nervous systems for frantic survival.*

- **Classification:** Type 3a (from Humans) or Type 4c (from Orcs).
- **Size:** **Small** (Stunted bone structure, hunched posture).
- **Speed:** 25 feet.
- **Vision:** Standard human vision (or low-light vision if native to deep Warren sumps).
- **Habitat & Breeding:** Goblins make up nearly 90% of the permanent underclass living in the deep Warrens of great spires. In zones of violent atmospheric flux (where Neither and Zone winds violently collide), goblins breed true. When isolated into stable atmospheric layers, their descendants gradually re-enter the standard generational adaptation cycle over several generations.
- **Heritage Features:**
  - **Flux Scavenger's Reflexes:** You possess hyper-reactive survival instincts born from dodging industrial machinery, collapsing shafts, and constabulary culls. You gain a +2 circumstance bonus to Reflex saving throws against traps and environmental hazards.
  - **Warren Gut:** Your stomach has adapted to survive on contaminated scraps, spoiled Mela flour, and raw Sycelium rot. You gain a +2 circumstance bonus on Fortitude saves against ingested poisons and diseases, and you treat food one class lower in cleanliness as if it were your native diet.
  - **Skulker of the Ducts:** Your stunted frame slips through service shafts, exhaust flues, and crawlspaces with ease. You can squeeze through tight spaces that would normally require a Tiny creature without treating them as difficult terrain.

### Rare Heritage: Vampire (Blood-Drinker / Sanguine Degenerate)
*Humans whose cellular metabolism has broken down, losing the capacity to absorb nutrition from standard Aither or spire crops. To arrest their agonizing physical decay, they must periodically harvest the lifeblood of living humanoids.*

- **Classification:** Type 3b.
- **Native Habitat:** Hidden throughout the middle and luxury wards of Great Spires. Because discovery brings instant summary execution by the King's Guard or exile into the lethal Neither wastes, vampires maintain meticulous public disguises as refined, perfumed nobles, wealthy merchants, or reclusive guild scholars.
- **The Dormant Curse:** A small number of standard humans carry a dormant strain of this metabolic mutation. Ingesting humanoid flesh or undergoing extreme necrotic tissue shock can trigger full vampiric activation.
- **Heritage Features:**
  - **Sanguine Hunger & Atrophy:** Standard food and drink provide you with zero metabolic sustenance; clean Aither food induces violent nausea. You must consume at least 1 pint of fresh blood from a Type 1, 2, or 3 human (Elf, Half-Elf, or Human) once every 7 days. 
    - Consuming **Orc blood (Type 4)** is thin and bitter, sustaining you for only 2 days per feeding.
    - If you go longer than 7 days without feeding, you become **Enfeebled 1** and **Drained 1**; these conditions increase by 1 each additional week without blood and cannot be removed until you feed.
  - **Predatory Senses & Fangs:** Your canines are elongated and your senses are wired to track pulsing arterial warmth. You gain an **unarmed Fangs Strike** that deals 1d6 piercing damage (Finesse, Agile). On a critical hit against a flat-footed humanoid target, you latch on and deal an extra 1d4 persistent bleed damage, satisfying half of your weekly blood requirement.
  - **Pallid Resilience:** Your halting metabolic pulse slows the spread of foreign toxins. You gain a +2 circumstance bonus on saving throws against non-ingested poisons and biological disease.

---

### Rare Heritage: Beast (Flesh-Gorger / Primal Outcast)
*Heavily built individuals—predominantly originating from Orc warbands or Lower Neither frontiers—whose brains suffer rapid, violent degradation of intellect, speech, and self-restraint unless satiated by raw humanoid flesh.*

- **Classification:** Type 4a.
- **Origin & Nature:** The biological mechanism that produces Beasts remains completely unknown to Spire scholars and Dwarven herbalists alike. Unlike vampires, who experience physical muscular withering, Beasts experience terrifying cognitive and emotional decay, descending into screaming, berserk frenzy if denied flesh.
- **Heritage Features:**
  - **Carnivorous Clarity:** To preserve your higher reasoning faculties, emotional equilibrium, and tactical restraint, you must consume raw humanoid flesh (Types 1 through 5) at least once every 14 days.
    - If you go longer than 14 days without humanoid flesh, your intellect rapidly degrades: you become **Stupefied 1** and take a –2 circumstance penalty on all Will saves against emotion effects and frenzy. Each subsequent week adds an additional level of Stupefied (up to Stupefied 3). Feeding immediately removes these conditions.
  - **Monstrous Physique:** Your frame is packed with dense, knotty muscle fiber. Your maximum Hit Points increase from 8 to **12**.
  - **Primal Claws:** Your fingernails and knuckles are thick, jagged bone spurs. You gain an **unarmed Claw Strike** dealing 1d6 slashing damage (Agile, Unarmed). When in a berserk fury or suffering from your Carnivorous Clarity penalty, your claws deal an additional 1 circumstance damage.

---

### Rare Heritage: Neanderthal (Neither-Starved Orc)
*Orcs whose bloodlines were cut off from Lower Neither radiation and fungal nourishment for extended periods, or who suffered permanent physiological trauma during deep famine.*

- **Classification:** Type 4b.
- **Biological Drift:** Neanderthals are not a distinct ancient race, but rather the scarred casualties of incomplete environmental transition. Deprived of Neither corruption, their heavy frames and thick skulls cannot properly sustain their bulk, leaving them with permanent physical and neurological trauma. Over several generations in clean air, their descendants smoothly transition into standard humans; conversely, returning to rich Neither food gradually restores their line to true Orc physiology.
- **Heritage Features:**
  - **Crude Ruggedness:** Despite your stunted, damaged nervous system, your thick skeletal structure shrugs off concussive blows. You gain physical resistance equal to 1 against bludgeoning damage and gain a +2 circumstance bonus to saving throws against being Stunned.
  - **Unyielding Survivalist:** Long exposure to starvation in transition frontiers has honed your basic instincts. You gain trained proficiency in Survival and Athletics, and gain the *Terrain Stalker* skill feat.
  - **Neither Re-Absorption:** Your body desperately hungers to restore its missing Neither saturation. When consuming Strata 4 raw Sycelium or traveling through dense Lower Neither fog, you gain a +1 circumstance bonus to Fortitude saves and recovery checks.

## 3. Global Demographics & Northern World Populations

The vast majority of the known civilized world centers around the **Northern Polar & Continental Shelf** (including the Kingdom of Arclean, Relocore, and the peripheral highland valleys). Humanity and Orcs represent nearly the entire humanoid population on the planet, with specialized strains holding strategic enclaves.

## 4. Geography of Human Settlement (Where Humans Live)

The human population (~500,000,000 Type 3 individuals) is distributed across five radically different environmental environments. Where a human lives dictates their social standing, longevity, dietary security, and physical health:

### 1. The Great Cities & Megalithic Spires (~87% of Humanity)
- **Living Conditions:** The overwhelming majority of civilized humanity resides within colossal spires of Neithercrete. Life varies enormously by altitude and ward circle:
  - **The Warrens (The Under-Spire):** Between **one-quarter (25%) and one-third (33%)** of every great city's residents live packed into the dark, damp Warrens behind the municipal circles. Life here is as brutal and short as the deepest convict mines, populated by indentured laborers, debtor clans, and an overwhelming majority of Goblins.
  - **Middle & Upper Wards:** Skilled artisans, guild masters, merchants, and nobility live in relative luxury, illuminated by wireless Zone-powered radiant street lamps, sheltered from Neither fog, and sustained by cultivated spire agriculture.
- **Mobility:** Many spire residents never set foot outside the city walls during their entire lifetimes. Movement between wards is tightly regulated by guarded transit paths and commercial checkpoints.

### 2. Hamlets & Satellite Settlements (~7.75% of Humanity)
- **Living Conditions:** These settlements range from fortified farming encampments of a few dozen souls to full walled towns without airship docking infrastructure. Some lie within visual sight of a great spire's searchlights, while others border the deep wilderness.
- **Vulnerability & Control:** Without airships or massive defensive arc cannons, hamlets exist under permanent threat of Lower Neither warbands and monster incursions. Nearly all are owned and operated by Spire Noble Houses (with larger towns claimed by two or three competing noble dynasties).
- **The Orc Drift:** Hamlets lack the hermetic sealing and high radiant grids of spires. Only a **constant supply of fresh spire-grown food** and a regular **rotation of laborers back into the city spires** prevents hamlet populations from biologically drifting into Orcs within two to three generations.

### 3. Island Communities (~3% of Humanity)
- **Living Conditions:** Populations settled on smaller islands in the outer oceans or isolated inland deep lakes. These communities enjoy an abundance of **deep-water ocean fish (caught below 150 ft)**, which provides clean, uncorrupted protein equivalent to high Aither food.
- **Land Ownership & Independence:** Unlike hamlet serfs who labor on noble estates, most islanders own their personal land and family fishing boats outright. City dwellers frequently romanticize island life as a pastoral paradise.
- **Isolation & Defense:** Mainland Orc warbands place zero culinary or cultural value on deep-water fish, and lack the maritime expertise to mount amphibious naval invasions; consequently, islands suffer virtually no raiding.
- **The Trade Lifeline:** Transport is strictly dependent on slow, unreliable wooden sailing ships. Islands near major sea trade lanes or within a day's sail of a coastal spire fall under spire municipal jurisdiction and receive daily or weekly dock traffic; isolated islands may see a single trade vessel once a year. If an island loses its access to deep-water fishing gear, its population shifts to Type 4 (Orcs) in just a few generations.

### 4. The Highlands (~1% of Humanity)
- **Living Conditions:** Perched along jagged mountain plateaus and high ridges sitting directly beneath the electrical boundary of the Zone.
- **Health vs. Technology:** Continuous exposure to direct sunlight and uncorrupted high-altitude game makes Highland humans some of the healthiest, tallest, and most physically admired specimens in the world. However, lacking the concentrated wealth of spire nobility and isolated from the radiant power grids that energize lower wards, highlanders live far more rugged, primitive, and clannish lives.
- **Raiding Societies:** Unlike Half-Elves who remain in high transition zones, Highland clans routinely travel down into the surface Neither valleys to hunt and raid before ascending back to their sunlit mountain homes.

### 5. The Depths & Mines (~1% of Humanity)
- **Living Conditions:** Populations living in deep subterranean shafts, excavation pits, and foundation quarries beneath the spires.
- **Demographics:** The most dangerous and physically punishing existence on the planet. The vast majority of deep miners are **serfs, debtors working off ancestral contracts, and penal convicts**.
- **Free Claims:** While freemen and freewomen can earn lucrative wages in mining operations, very few willingly accept the subterranean life. A rare minority operate independent "miner claims"—though nobility routinely seizes or claims the richest mineral veins, leaving private miners with low-yield, dangerous scrap tunnels.

## 5. Modern Advances: Airship Expansion & Long-Range Contact

Over the past half-century, technological revolutions in airship engineering have reshaped the known world:

- **50 Years Ago (Long-Range Range Expansion):** Crucial innovations in radiant engine efficiency and storm-stabilized envelope design massively extended airship operational range (though flight speeds remained relatively static along the Zone plane). For the first time, regular trade routes connected distant continents and mountain kingdoms that were previously known only through ancient folklore, as no navigable land or water passage crossed the intervening Neither miasmas.
- **10 Years Ago (Speed & Long-Distance Breakthroughs):** Further engineering breakthroughs enhanced propulsion velocity and sustained high-altitude endurance. 
- **Recent Reports (First Contact with Distant Continents):** Expeditions traveling months beyond the standard Arclean maritime charts have recently made contact with civilizations so remote they had never seen an airship or heard of the Great Spires. These distant realms possess alien resources, ancient pre-collapse ruins, and untapped markets, sparking a fierce geopolitical race among Great Spire merchant cartels and naval armadas.
