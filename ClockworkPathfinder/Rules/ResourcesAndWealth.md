## 1. Core Philosophy: Institutional Backing vs. Personal Coin

In the Great Spires of Clockwork Cities, personal pocket cash is secondary to institutional credit and backing (*"Worth = who you represent"*). Wealth is divided into **Institutional Backing (Permanent Wealth)** and **Personal Allowances (Transitory Wealth)**:
- **Storyteller Guidance, Not Universal Cutoffs:** Currency norms, toll examples, wealth thresholds, and social-reaction examples in this document are practical guidelines for building a consistent setting, not a complete price list or fixed legal mechanics. The Storyteller may adapt them to the story, local ward, and characters; decide how much scrutiny, resistance, or consequence a situation draws; and create a distinct local economy or social order where useful. The King's authority remains absolute within the setting: his word is law, while society shapes the likely consequences of how he exercises it.
- **Scope & Default Setting:** These wealth rules are chiefly calibrated for play in **Ward 13, the Military, the other Wards, and the University**. These settings are generally middle-class to upper-middle-class, with frequent visits from the truly wealthy. This is a practical baseline for those environments, not a claim that every resident has the same means; the truly wealthy and the destitute of the Inner Warrens need distinct treatment. **Ward 13 Warrens have their own special rules in Section 3; Inner Warrens have their own rules in Section 4.**
- **Price, Quality & Social Signaling:** Price differences between wealth zones reflect both real differences in quality and the King's higher taxes on goods, as well as higher rents and labor costs in wealthier zones. Wealth and quality form a spectrum, not a set of sharp tiers: the finest goods affordable within one stratum may look much like the cheapest goods sold in the next, and neighboring strata overlap in materials, workmanship, style, and price. Most people can make a rough estimate of an item's quality and likely market, but cannot reliably infer its owner's status from one item alone. Owning or wearing goods from another stratum is legal; any social reaction depends on context and the wearer's circumstances, and people close to moving up or down may be hard to distinguish from one another. Constables and the King's Guard shut down businesses engaged in deceptive imitation or fraud, not legitimate lower-cost goods whose quality naturally overlaps with higher-priced wares. Within a zone, residents can usually find establishments offering goods or services around one currency level below or above the local norm, serving those barely making ends meet as well as customers aspiring to move up or afford the highest standard their ward supports.
- **Average Ward Resident & Social Standing:** Money visibly signals standing. In the slum wards, Slivers pay for most purchases, while Pins are common for small transactions and usually overlooked by constables. For an average civilian ward resident, a hoard above 200 Pins may attract attention, and more than 3 Labor coins is suspect because Labors are hard to spend locally. These are social-enforcement expectations, not universal possession laws: government, military, and law-enforcement personnel may carry authorized funds without the same restrictions and often live or work in separate areas or higher wards.
- **Worker, Upper-Worker & Luxury Ward Currency:** In the Worker Wards (7–9) and worker-accessible areas of the Sky Docks, ordinary meals, food, and restaurant purchases are commonly priced and paid in Workers; Slivers cover small incidental purchases. In upper Worker and luxury wards, Workers remain common for ordinary meals and service, but Slivers replace Pins as the lowest commonly used denomination. Pins are looked down on in these areas, though **10–15 Pins** are generally overlooked and may be used to pay children for minor tasks. The wealthiest districts and transactions commonly use Nobels.
- **Military Ward, University & Ward 13 Mixed Currency:** These districts mix residents, workers, students, and visitors from lower wards. Most ordinary prices are in Nobels, with Labors commonly used as change. Slivers are treated as spare change and are readily accepted by children for small tasks. Because people travel here from lower wards, Pins still circulate; carrying **more than about 10 Pins** is suspicious. Food and restaurant prices vary widely by quality: a basic meal brought up by a worker may cost Slivers, while the finest dining may cost Princes.
- **Merchant Ward Currency & Status:** The Merchant Ward's ordinary prices are based in Royals. Wall Merchants and Greater Nobles typically reckon their own purchases in Princes; major trade may be valued in Princes and Sovereigns. Royalty follows this Prince-based convention when actually paying with money. Wall Merchants, Greater Nobles, and royalty may refuse Workers as change, considering the denomination beneath their station. Slivers are beneath the station of Wall Merchants and lesser or base Nobles and their households, so they generally do not bother with them as change. They may keep 5–10 Slivers to reward children for minor errands, but commonly tell others not to bother and to keep the change. Handling Slivers for ordinary purchases is socially degrading; Pins are unthinkable in public.
- **Durable vs. Fragile Institutional Wealth:**
  - *Noble Wealth:* Durable, anchored in royal land grants, deep-vein mines, and Crown patents. If a noble estate burns down, the Crown helps rebuild.
  - *Merchant Wealth:* Liquid and fragile, anchored in airship cargoes, bills of exchange, and warehouse goods. A lost convoy can bankrupt a merchant house overnight.
- **Institutional Roleplay, Storyteller Final Authority & The 3-Tier Decision Structure:**
  - **Members, Not Rulers (Until High Level):** Players are **members** of a House or Guild—until high level, **they will not run them**.
  - **No Macro-Level Cost/Gain Micromanagement (Design Intention):** By deliberate design, **players never micromanage costs, leases, square footage, or financial gains at a larger institutional or property level**. Any granular mathematical system for property rent, commercial revenue, or facility size inevitably creates exploitable loopholes and traps the Storyteller into answering endless administrative questions (*"Exactly how much floor space do we need?"*). Instead, housing, workspaces, and institutional backing are handled **abstractly through Status, Standing, and roleplay**.
  - **Roleplay & Storyteller Final Word:** Many resource and institutional questions (what an organization believes a character needs for a given task, how returned wealth is rewarded, or what is approved) are resolved through **roleplay between the player and the DM / Storyteller**, and **it is the Storyteller—not the player—who has the final word** on what is and is not. Players are free not to play or to make their own game if they dislike the decisions the Storyteller is making; equally, Storytellers need to keep the players interested enough in the game.
  - **The 3-Tier Decision Structure:** All rules and Storyteller decisions follow three clear tiers:
    1. **Tier 1 (Highest) — Fun & Beneficial to the Game:** *Is it fun/beneficial to the game?* If not, why are we doing it this way?
    2. **Tier 2 — Theme & Mood:** *Does it fit the theme and mood of the game world?* (Not as important as Rule 1).
    3. **Tier 3 (Lowest) — Realism & Rules Alignment:** *Is it realistic and aligned with other rules in the game?* Ideally yes, **but can be ignored if Rules 1 and 2 are being met**.
- **No "Off-the-Books" Laundering in the Standard Spire Economy:** True off-the-books cash hoarding does not exist in the ordinary institutional economy:
  - **Civilized Wards:** Your Family House, Trade Guild, University, or Military unit knows precisely what you should earn and own. Private, anonymous secure vaults do not exist, and no constable or court will investigate the theft of wealth that legally should not exist (and admitting you possessed it risks immediate expulsion or criminal charges).
  - **Organized Crime in the Wards (Legitimate Fronts):** Crime families and organized crime inside the Wards are tied to legitimate fronts (smaller guilds, taverns, bars, restaurants). Members live within the means of a more profitable version of that same legitimate group, and society treats them as that social class. As long as they do not cause too much trouble or decrease the Spire's wealth, they are tolerated; their resource streams (Monthly Spending, Personal Property Cap, Requisitioned Gear) operate through that legitimate front's status tier.
  - **Inner Warrens (Levels 1–12):** These are not part of the ordinary institutional economy. Residents are destitute; barter is most common, Pins circulate in small amounts, and Slivers are extraordinary wealth. Their subsistence and routes into the Spire are defined separately in Section 4.

## 2. The Five Player Resource Categories

Every character's access to money, equipment, and personnel is governed by five distinct resource categories determined by their **Group Status**, **Role**, and **Internal Standing**:

### 1. Monthly Spending Allowance (Discretionary & Excess Expenses Only)
- **All Expected Lifestyle Costs Absorbed by the Organization:** Your sponsoring institution directly covers all normal lifestyle expenses appropriate to a person of your status tier and role. Dining at restaurants of your status tier (or below), boarding in appropriate quarters, standard daily rations, uniforms/clothing, routine carriage travel, and even hosting or treating a small party of colleagues/guests within reason **is automatically charged to the organization's ledger and never costs personal coin**.
  - **Spire Leases & Group Housing (Setting Lore, Not Player Accounting):** In the world of the Spires, the cost of leasing any location follows a strict in-universe formula (with a regulated surcharge on wealth generation), with the managing Nobles collecting rent and finding new tenants when spaces are empty. Each Ward—and where in the Ward a property sits—defines its cost, and most people live in apartments supplied by their guild or family. **Mechanically, this is purely narrative worldbuilding:** players never calculate rent formulas, square footage, or property surcharges at the table—your housing and workspace quality simply reflect your group's current Status and your Standing within it.
- **When Personal Spending is Used:** Your liquid **Monthly Spending Allowance** is spent only when you choose to **live beyond your station** (e.g., a Status 2 laborer booking a luxury private box in Ward 13), pay bribes, stake personal gambling bets, or purchase unauthorized black-market goods.

### 2. Personal Property Cap (`1 × Monthly Spending Limit`)
- **Strict Personal Ceiling:** With core combat equipment exempted by *The Citizen Bulwark* (see Category 5 below) and operational mission gear supplied by your organization (see Category 3 below), players may keep personal items and pocket coin **only up to their Monthly Spending Limit (`1 × Monthly Spending`)**.
- **Surplus Wealth Disposal:** All personal wealth, coin, or unexempted items above your `1 × Monthly Spending Limit` (excluding your 3 *Citizen Bulwark* items and items formally granted for *exceptional service to house or crown*) cannot be kept:
  - **Aligned Citizens (Returned to Organization):** All wealth above this limit is automatically returned to the organization you are part of (House, Guild, University, or Military treasury), which may increase your **Standing** depending on the current wealth and supply needs of the organization.
  - **Unaligned Individuals (Stolen or Spent):** If a character is unaligned—or attempts to hide surplus wealth from their organization—all wealth above their Monthly Spending Limit is rapidly **stolen** (by thieves, extortionists, or corrupt officials) or **spent** on fleeting luxuries, vice, food, and drink.
- **Gambling Stakes & Windfalls:**
  - *Gambling Stakes Rule:* If a character intends to keep gambling winnings as personal wealth, the initial stakes **must be staked out of personal spending cash**, never organizational credit. If house/guild credit is used to gamble, all winnings belong to the house ledger.
  - *Windfall Disposal:* Any gambling streak or illicit score pushing a character above their `1 × Monthly Spending Limit` must be spent immediately on consumables/favors, returned to their organization, or lost to theft/vice before the month ends.

### 3. Requisitioned Equipment (The Institutional Library & Task Exemption)
- **Personal Limits Never Apply to Organizational Mission Gear:** The `1 × Monthly Spending` Personal Property Cap and Citizen Bulwark limits **never apply to items that your organization can afford and believes you need to perform your role or given tasks**.
- **Adaptable Institutional Library:** Rather than fixed purchases, organizational equipment functions as an adaptable library. Whenever you return to your house estate, guildhall, or military barracks, you can requisition and swap out equipment appropriate to your role and upcoming assignment (e.g., drawing a heavy trench respirator, diagnostic array, or masterwork tools for an expedition).
- **Bound by Organizational Profile & Means:** An organization can only issue what it can afford and what fits its institutional profile (e.g., a Blacksmith's Guild can issue masterwork metallurgy and pneumatic tools, but cannot issue an elven gunpowder dueling pistol; a Great House armory can issue ancestral plate and rapiers, but does not stock illegal Warrens gas grenades).
- **Strict Organizational Ownership:** **All requisitioned items always belong to the organization, never to the player.**
  - They cannot be sold or permanently gifted by the player.
  - High-status PCs may temporarily loan a requisitioned item to a party ally for a mission, but the item legally remains the organization's property and is governed by the borrower's accountability to that house/guild.
  - If a character's Standing drops, their task concludes, or they leave their post, these items are immediately recalled. Losing requisitioned equipment is judged by the organization's quartermaster based on the item's rarity/cost and whether it was lost in honorable service versus negligence.

### 4. Retainer Budget (Subordinates Only & Monthly Reallocation)
- **Available to High-Status Roles:** Granted to Nobles, Merchants, and Military/Constabulary Commanders for hiring, equipping, and provisioning household guards, runners, clerks, and apprentice hirelings (including hiring lower-status PCs under a retainer contract, provided the player agrees and the gear fits the task).
- **Cannot Be Used Directly by the PC:** Retainer Budget funds and equipment are strictly earmarked for subordinates and **can never be spent on or used directly by the PC for their own personal gear, upkeep, or expenses**.
- **Mandatory Monthly Reallocation:** Retainer budgets do not roll over or accumulate from month to month; they **must be reallocated every month**.

### 5. The Citizen Bulwark (3-Item PC Upkeep & Resource Exemption)
- **Lore & Legal History:** Clockwork and NeitherTeck technologies require constant calibration, lubrication, and costly spare parts—ongoing upkeep far beyond what most residents of the Spire could ever afford under their personal spending limits. This is offset by one of the oldest surviving laws from the dawn of the Spire era: **The Citizen Bulwark**.
  - **The Statutory Grant:** The law grants skilled citizens municipal and institutional funding and spare-parts access to maintain **3 items of personal gear** (traditionally framed as *1 suit of protective armor, 1 primary weapon, and 1 additional clockwork/defense item*).
  - **The Militia Anachronism:** Under the ancient text of the Bulwark, each participating citizen agrees to serve in the Spire Militia in times of need. *(More recent Crown laws have long since codified that militia service is a mandatory requirement of ALL citizens during a crisis anyway—the fact that the Citizen Bulwark still frames militia duty as a voluntary agreement shows just how old the law is).*
- **Mechanical Budget Rule (Any 3 Items Outside Spire Limits — Player's Choice):**
  - While citizens traditionally select 1 armor, 1 weapon, and 1 clockwork defense item, **this breakdown is not mandatory**.
  - **Player Freedom:** If a player chooses to maintain **3 weapons**, **3 pieces of specialized gear/apparatus**, or any random assortment of equipment that fits their character concept, **the rules explicitly allow it**. Players are never forced into an optimal combat trinity; a dedicated scholar, sniper, or artisan can freely dedicate their 3 slots to whatever tools, secondary blades, or gear they cherish.
  - Every PC keeps and maintains **any 3 designated personal items completely outside of the normal resource limits in the Spire** (exempt from the `1 × Monthly Spending` Personal Property Cap, ongoing upkeep drain, and routine organizational repossession).
- **Level-Adjusted Price & PF2e Progression Alignment (`[GM Core p.58]`):**
  - The allowed price and tier of each of the 3 Citizen Bulwark items is **adjusted by Character Level (supporting permanent items up to Item Level = Character Level)** and aligns directly with the normal Pathfinder 2e permanent magic item progression (`[GM Core p.58, Table 2–10]`).
  - This ensures every PC—regardless of social class or personal spending cap—can maintain level-appropriate fundamental enhancements (`+1 Niracrest`, `Striking`, `Resilient`, `+2 Mitheral`, `Greater Striking`, etc.) and clockwork upgrades as they advance in level.
  - *(Note: Standard municipal carry, peace-bonding, and cartridge/converter checkout laws in `WorldAndLaw.md` still apply in public wards, and items above the Level 12 retail store ceiling must still be acquired via expeditions, vaults, or commissions).*

### 6. Institutional Gifts & Exceptional Service Dispensations (Items Far Beyond Wealth)

One foundational question of the Spire economy is: **How do characters acquire and permanently keep items whose value dwarfs their personal wealth and social standing?**

Because characters cannot hoard surplus coin under the `1 × Monthly Spending` cap, top-tier items are almost never acquired through ordinary retail shopping. Instead, they are bestowed as **Institutional Gifts and Royal Dispensations for exceptional service**:

- **Service as the True Currency:** Exceptional service rendered to a **Trade Guild**, a **Great Noble House**, a **High Collegium**, the **Municipal Watch**, or directly to the **Crown / King** is rewarded through formal honorific grants:
  - **"In the King's Name":** Prior to high tier (Levels 1–9), imperial dispensations are issued *in the King's name* by ward bailiffs, garrison colonels, or chancellor magistrates. Only when players enter high-tier play (**Level 10+**) do rewards stem directly from an audience with the Sovereign himself or the Privy Council.
- **The Two Forms of Institutional Gifts:**
  1. **The Sovereign Right of Retention (Keep-Right Patent):**
     - When a character recovers a rare, legendary, or pre-collapse Era 1 artifact during an expedition—an item that legally belongs to the Crown or the sponsoring institution—the granting authority issues a formal **Keep-Right Patent** (signed and stamped with municipal/royal seals).
     - This patent grants the PC the permanent legal right to own, maintain, and carry the item despite their lower social station. Constables, guild assayers, and tax collectors must recognize the patent; any attempt by lower officials to seize it is treated as royal theft.
  2. **The "Bespoke Patronage" Store Selection:**
     - The benefactor issues a **Letter of Introduction & Patronage** directing the character to a specific, high-tier artisan workshop, master guild salon, or private noble factorum.
     - Upon arrival, the shop proprietor—already compensated or commanded by the patron—greets the character, closes the doors to the public, and presents a curated selection of exceptional masterworks.
     - The character is invited to select an item of their choice as a formal token of gratitude. The transaction involves zero personal coin from the PC; the invoice is settled entirely through the patron's institutional treasury or royal debt credits.
- **Cross-Institutional Patronage (Third-Party Gifts):**
  - An institutional gift **does not have to come from your own organization**. A working-class Soldier or Warrens Survivor who saves a Wall Merchant’s heir, uncovers an assassination plot against a Great House, or defends an Artisan guildhall from saboteurs can receive a gift from that outside faction.
  - **Immunity from Own House Repossession:** Because the gift is backed by an outside entity's formal decree or Crown warrant, the PC's own house, guild, or regiment **cannot legally confiscate or repossess the item** into the general house library. It remains the personal, recognized property of the individual who earned it.
- **Integration with the Citizen Bulwark Exception (The King's Exemption):**
  - **Standard Institutional Gifts Consume a Citizen Bulwark Slot:** When a Trade Guild, a Noble House, an individual patron, or an outside benefactor gives you an item far beyond your wealth, **it must occupy one of your 3 designated Citizen Bulwark slots** to be retained personally. (While these 3 slots are traditionally 1 armor, 1 weapon, and 1 defense item, a player may freely allocate them to any 3 items they choose, including multiple weapons or non-combat gear). If your 3 Bulwark slots are already full, you must choose which item to surrender/replace, or the new gift must be deposited into your house/guild library.
  - **The Royal Exemption (Granted by or in the Name of the King):** The sole legal exception occurs when an item is **granted directly by the King, or formally bestowed in the King's Name by a royal bailiff or high magistrate**. A royal dispensation carries sovereign authority that transcends municipal statute: it **exists completely outside and in addition to your 3 Citizen Bulwark slots**, granting a permanent extra retention slot that cannot be taxed, confiscated, or contested by any guild or house.

## 3. Special Rules: Ward 13 Warrens

The luxury underworld behind Ward 13, run by Johnny Ralen and Amanda Coren, is a sanctioned social escape valve, not an extension of the ordinary Ward economy or the destitute Inner Warrens. It mirrors Ward 13's three-circle structure in three concentric levels and is the first Warrens district where visitors can readily walk across the spire.

- **Class Structure & Institutional Expectations:** The Warrens' social and criminal organizations have rigid class structures and institutional expectations. Operators know which lines they can and cannot cross.
- **Three Levels & Price Tiers:**
  - **First Ring:** Caters to Ward 13 residents and visitors, with prices and establishments aligned to Ward 13.
  - **Second Level:** After paying the access toll, visitors reach a level catering to those with fewer resources; prices and establishments align with the Labor Wards.
  - **Top Level:** Caters to the ultra-rich, with prices and establishments mirroring the Nobel and Merchant Wards, including venues specifically serving Wall Merchants and Greater Nobles.
- **Citizenship & Resource Limits:** Ward 13 syndicate operators and Black Marketeers who hold legal citizenship are subject to the same citizenship and resource limits as other citizens; the district does not bypass the standard wealth rules.
- **Pins & Enforcement Elsewhere:** Pins are unofficial scrip and standard merchants reject them for ordinary listed goods, but they are common for small purchases and debts in slum wards. Constables generally overlook ordinary use. For an average civilian ward resident, more than 200 Pins may attract questioning or confiscation, while possessing more than 3 Labor coins is suspect because Labors are difficult to spend locally. These are informal social-enforcement expectations, not universal legal limits; authorized government, military, and law-enforcement funds are treated differently. The Inner Warrens have a separate subsistence economy.
  - **Worker Wards & Sky Dock Workers:** In Wards 7–9 and worker-accessible Sky Dock areas, Workers are the usual denomination for food, meals, and restaurants, with Slivers for small purchases. In upper Worker and luxury wards, Slivers replace Pins as the lowest commonly used denomination, while Nobels are common in the wealthiest districts and transactions. Pins carry social stigma in these areas, but 10–15 are generally overlooked and may be used for minor child errands. A street vendor's business till may contain around 200 Pins without drawing attention; do not treat that business float as a worker's personal coin.
  - **Military Ward, University & Ward 13:** Most prices are in Nobels, with Labors common as change; Slivers are spare change and children readily accept them for minor tasks. Pins arrive with lower-ward visitors but more than about 10 on a person is suspicious. Food and restaurant costs range from Slivers for basic worker-supplied food to Princes for the finest dining.
  - **High-Status Exemption & Social Penalty:** Constables do not search or confiscate ordinary currency from high-status citizens (Nobles, Wall Merchants, senior guild masters, commissioned officers). However, Wall Merchants, lesser or base Nobles, Greater Nobles, and royalty view carrying Pins or handling Slivers with severe disdain. Wall Merchants, Greater Nobles, and royalty may also refuse Workers as change. Like Wall Merchants, lesser or base Nobles may keep 5–10 Slivers to reward children, but commonly tell others to keep Sliver change. On-duty personal servants embody their master's honor; handling Slivers or Pins in an official capacity dishonors a noble patron. Pins are present on noble estates only secretly belowstairs or when carried covertly by family spies.
  - **Spire-Wide Small Favors & Oversight:** Across the ordinary Spire wards, Pins may pay children for small errands or favors too minor to justify a Sliver. The King's Guard tacitly monitors Pin volume to let the poorest trade and survive without stabilizing a parallel formal economy.

## 4. Special Rules: Inner Warrens (Levels 1–12)

The Inner Warrens are the land of the destitute, distinct from the Ward 13 Warrens' luxury vice district. Their residents work to survive rather than participate in the Spire's normal coin economy.

- **Subsistence & Work:** Mushroom-vat labor can provide a place to sleep and access to food. Cash wages, when paid, may be only a few Pins per week.
- **Barter & Currency:** Most transactions are barter. Pins are the currency most commonly found, but only in small amounts; even 10 Pins marks someone as doing exceptionally well. A Sliver is unmanageable wealth for most residents, not an everyday coin. Do not apply ordinary Ward budgets or prices to Inner Warrens residents.
- **Self-Funded Buyout:** A healthy person with at least **10 Slivers** in wealth can buy out of the Inner Warrens and enter the Spire. This sum is an enormous threshold in the Inner Warrens, not a routine fare.
- **Four-Year Bonded Exit:** The fittest and most skilled residents may instead knowingly accept a four-year service bond. An employer pays the King for the bond, and the resident is effectively sold into that term of service. A person capable and astute enough to understand the agreement may accept it; on completing the four years, they enter the slum district as a Freeman.
- **Power Containment:** The **Military, King's Guard, and Constabulary** use covert and overt means to prevent Inner Warrens factions from accumulating enough power to threaten the Spire.

## 5. Currency System (Industrial Hard-Alloy Standard)

The official currency of Arclean and the Great Spires is not made of soft precious metals (gold or silver) easily clipped or scraped, but of an **extremely hard industrial alloy** manufactured through specialized high-temperature metallurgical pressing.

### Physical Nature & Anti-Counterfeiting
- **High-Temperature Molded & Pressed:** Official coins (**Sliver** through **Sovereign**) can only be molded and pressed by massive industrial factory equipment at extreme temperatures. This makes duplication virtually impossible without access to crown-controlled municipal forges.
- **Hard & Shatter-Prone:** The metal is extraordinarily hard and wear-resistant under normal friction, but brittle under catastrophic shock. If struck with sufficient shearing or percussive force (e.g., a heavy hammer blow or a crushing hydraulic vice), the coins will **shatter rather than bend**.
- **Distinctive Alloy Metallurgy:** Each denomination is minted using a distinct metallic alloy formula, giving every tier an unmistakable coloration, surface luster, ring tone, and density that makes counterfeit coins instantly recognizable to merchants, assayers, and constables.

### Converting Standard Pathfinder 2e Equipment & Costs
- **Direct Parity Formula:**
  - **1 Copper Piece (cp) = 1 Sliver (Sl)**
  - **1 Silver Piece (sp) = 1 Worker (W)**
  - **1 Gold Piece (gp) = 1 Labor (L)**
  - **10 Gold Pieces (10 gp / 1 pp) = 1 Nobel (N)**
  - **100 Gold Pieces (100 gp / 10 pp) = 1 Royal (Roy)**
  - **1,000 Gold Pieces (1,000 gp / 100 pp) = 1 Prince (Pr)**
  - **10,000 Gold Pieces (10,000 gp / 1,000 pp) = 1 Sovereign (Sov)**
- **No Pin Pricing on Standard Goods:** No standard listed item or equipment in the catalogs is ever priced in Pins. All commercial listings begin at Slivers.
